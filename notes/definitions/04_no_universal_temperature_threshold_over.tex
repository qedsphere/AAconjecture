\subsection{No universal temperature threshold over all Gibbs measures}

Write $\mu_{n,\beta}(x)\propto\exp[-\beta H_n(x)]$. Multiplying $H_n$ by $a>0$ replaces $\beta$ by $a\beta$, so a numerical transition requires an energy normalization. Every strictly positive distribution $\nu$ on a finite cube is Gibbs at any prescribed $\beta_0>0$: take $H(x)=-\log\nu(x)/\beta_0$.

The constant Hamiltonian gives uniform inputs at every temperature. Independent-field Hamiltonians give biased product inputs, which satisfy distributional AA at every fixed finite bias by \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35327}{Girish--Mittal--Nehoran--Raz}, combined with uniform AA.

Concentration alone is not decisive. Concentration on a single configuration makes the variance of every bounded observable vanish. The Curie--Weiss counterexample instead exploits two separated phases retaining nonzero weights.
