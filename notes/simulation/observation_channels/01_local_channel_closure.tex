\subsection{An exact local posterior sampler for observation channels}
\label{sec:resolution-channel-closure}

The high-temperature theorem in
Section~\ref{resolution-dynamics:theorem} survives arbitrary independent
observation noise. The relevant posterior has a special property:
conditioning on the observations changes only single-site fields.
The proof below establishes the required input-query bound, including
adaptive requests; an efficient unconditional sampler would not suffice.

\begin{theorem}[Local observation channels preserve high-temperature full AA]
\label{thm:resolution-channel-closure}
Let \(S\in\{-1,+1\}^n\) have a classical Gibbs law
\[
 \pi(s)\propto\exp\left(
       \beta\sum_{A:\,|A|\ge2}J_A\prod_{i\in A}s_i
       +\beta\sum_i h_i s_i\right).
\]
Let the graph joining sites that occur together in an interaction have
maximum degree at most a fixed \(D\), and put
\[
 \Lambda=\max_i\sum_{A\ni i,\,|A|\ge2}|J_A|,
 \qquad \theta=D(1-e^{-4\beta\Lambda}).
\]
Assume either \(\theta\le\theta_*<1\), with fixed margin, or that
all interactions have size at most a fixed \(k\ge2\) and
\(\beta\Lambda\le1/[8(k-1)]\).
Given \(S\), let \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\) be independent, with arbitrary
known binary channels \(K_i(x_i\mid S_i)\). The channels may depend on
\(n\) and may contain zero probabilities. Then the law of \(X\)
satisfies full AA with query bound
\(\operatorname{poly}_{D,k,\theta_*}(T,1/e)\), with irrelevant
parameters omitted in the respective case, uniformly over all
channels, fields, signs, and numbers of sites.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each site introduce independent table bits
\(B_i(+1),B_i(-1)\), with
\(\Pr[B_i(t)=x]=K_i(x\mid t)\). All table bits are mutually
independent and independent of \(S\). Set
\[
 Z=(S,B),\qquad F(Z)_i=B_i(S_i).
\]
The source law of \(Z\) is a Gibbs law with the original interactions
on \(S\), plus isolated independent spins. A deterministic table bit
can be removed and hardwired. Every other table bias is represented by
a finite single-spin field. For clarity, represent this source at
inverse temperature one, with dimensionless interaction couplings
\(\beta J_A\) and arbitrary dimensionless table fields; this also
covers \(\beta=0\). Thus
Theorem~\ref{thm:resolution-dynamics-ising}, including its stated
higher-order extension, or
Theorem~\ref{thm:resolution-dynamics-weighted} in the second regime,
gives a deterministic source simulator of cost
\(s=s_{\rm dyn}(6T,e/2)\). The factor six suffices to read the three
source bits needed for one output and uncompute the workspace.
This is a coherent oracle implementation on the entire source cube.
There is no forward approximation: \(X=F(Z)\) exactly.

Fix an observed configuration \(x\) of positive probability. Its
latent posterior is
\[
 \pi_x(s):=\Pr[S=s\mid X=x]
 \propto \pi(s)\prod_i K_i(x_i\mid s_i).
 \tag{LP}
\]
When both likelihoods are positive, the additional dimensionless
field at site \(i\) is
\(\frac12\log[K_i(x_i\mid+1)/K_i(x_i\mid-1)]\).
If exactly one likelihood is zero, that site is pinned. A positive
probability \(x\) cannot give two zero likelihoods at the same site.
Consequently \(\pi_x\) has the same interaction graph and no larger
incident interaction strength, even if its fields become arbitrarily
large. Crucially, the new local field at \(i\) is determined by
one query to \(x_i\).

\textbf{The posterior random-map construction.}
Fix the proper-color schedule used in
Section~\ref{resolution-dynamics:theorem}, repeated at all negative
times. Assign independent uniform variables to every site-update event.
Use the heat-bath threshold map for \(\pi_x\) at that event.
The probability that such a map is nonconstant is at most
\[
 p:=\tanh(\beta\Lambda).
 \tag{ACT}
\]
Indeed, the possible dimensionless local fields lie in an interval
\([a-\beta\Lambda,a+\beta\Lambda]\); the difference between the
largest and smallest probabilities of spin \(+1\) is maximized at
\(a=0\), where it equals \(\tanh(\beta\Lambda)\).
Pinned sites have constant update maps. Put \(\alpha=Dp\).
For \(u\ge0\), \(\tanh u\le1-e^{-4u}\), so
\(\alpha\le\theta_*<1\).

To produce a requested terminal spin \(S_i\), inspect its last update
event and read \(x_i\) if not already known. If its map is constant,
that spin is determined. Otherwise recursively request all its at most
\(D\) neighbor spins immediately before the event. This explores
strictly decreasing update times. Cache inspected random maps,
computed spin values, and input values across all requests. Every
fresh event adds at most \(D\) predecessor requests, and its conditional
expected number of new requests is at most \(\alpha\).

Here this expectation remains valid during arbitrary adaptive source
queries: a requested source value is returned only after its complete
dependency exploration has been resolved. The values already returned,
the next requested coordinate, and the work queue are functions of
previously inspected uniform variables and the fixed input \(x\).
The uniform variable at a previously uninspected event is therefore
still independent of that history. Already inspected events create no
new work. This uses independence in a freshly constructed posterior
sampler; it does not assume that a forward source map retains its
unconditional law after observing the final input.

In the second temperature regime use the signed-parity random maps of
Lemma~\ref{lem:resolution-dynamics-parity-law} in place of threshold
maps. Read \(x_i\) to determine the posterior field and hence the
local map distribution, then use the fresh uniform variable to select
its finite categorical map. Only the chosen support is requested.
The unconditioned expected support size is at most the same uniform
bound as its conditional expectation in that lemma, namely
\(\alpha=160/169<1\). Its distribution is unaffected by past
exploration for the same fresh-event reason. All remaining statements
below use this \(\alpha\). Thus they cover both regimes.

If at most \(s\) terminal latent requests are made, and \(N\) is
the total number of distinct inspected update events, the queue bound
gives
\[
 \mathbb E[N\mid x]\le \frac{s}{1-\alpha}.
 \tag{WORK}
\]
For completeness, stop after at most \(M\) inspected events. At most
\(s\) roots have been added. The number of inspected events is at
most this root count plus the number of children created. Taking
expectations and using the fresh-event bound yields
\((1-\alpha)\mathbb E[\min(N,M)\mid x]\le s\).
Letting \(M\) increase proves (WORK) and almost-sure termination.

The resulting terminal spins have joint law \(\pi_x\). To see this,
start a stationary \(\pi_x\) chain at a finite time in the past and
use the same maps up to time zero. On the event that the explored
ancestors of the finitely many requested sites stay later than the
initial time, the returned spins do not depend on the initial state.
Almost-sure finite exploration lets that initial time tend to minus
infinity. Equivalently, this is a local coupling-from-the-past
construction. It defines a single global posterior sample whose
coordinates can be revealed adaptively.

\textbf{Completing the source posterior.}
After sampling \(S_i\), a request for \(B_i(t)\) is answered as
follows. If \(t=S_i\), its value is the observed \(x_i\). Otherwise
sample it with its original channel law, independently of everything
else. Cache this answer. Conditional on \(S\) and \(X=x\), precisely
the selected entry of each channel table is constrained, so this gives
the exact law of \(Z\mid X=x\). Each source-bit request requires
at most one terminal latent-spin request and at most one extra query
to \(x\), besides the update-event exploration.

Run the source simulator with this posterior oracle, but abort its
exploration after
\[
 M=\left\lceil\frac{2s}{e(1-\alpha)}\right\rceil
\]
update events, returning zero if the cap is exceeded. By (WORK) and
Markov's inequality the conditional abort probability is at most
\(e/2\), for every positive-probability \(x\), even under the
simulator's adaptive source requests. There are at most \(M+s\)
physical input queries. The exact, uncapped procedure has average
error at most \(e/2\), because it supplies the exact source posterior
and \(X=F(Z)\). Capping therefore gives average error at most \(e\).
All uniform variables and table coins can be fixed in advance
independently of \(x\). Averaging fixes them to obtain a deterministic
simulator with the claimed error and query bound.
\end{proof}

The coupling-from-the-past principle originates with
\href{https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/206-RWG/RWGpapers/propp_wilson.pdf}
{Propp and Wilson (1996)}. The extra assertion needed here is the
uniform physical-input query bound for the posterior and adaptive
coordinate requests; the proof above supplies it explicitly.
