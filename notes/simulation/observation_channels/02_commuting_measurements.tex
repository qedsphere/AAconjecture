\subsection{A quantum Gibbs consequence with explicit basis dependence}
\label{sec:resolution-commuting-measurements}

\begin{corollary}[Product-basis measurements of locally diagonalizable Gibbs states]
\label{cor:resolution-commuting-measurements}
For each qubit let \(N_i\) be a Hermitian operator with eigenvalues
\(\{-1,+1\}\), and consider
\[
 H=-\sum_{A:\,|A|\ge2}J_A\prod_{i\in A}N_i-\sum_i h_iN_i.
\]
Suppose the interaction graph has maximum degree at most a fixed
\(D\), and define
\(\Lambda=\max_i\sum_{A\ni i,\,|A|\ge2}|J_A|\).
If
\[
 D(1-e^{-4\beta\Lambda})\le\theta_*<1,
\]
or, for interactions of size at most fixed \(k\), if
\(\beta\Lambda\le1/[8(k-1)]\),
then measuring \(e^{-\beta H}/\operatorname{tr}(e^{-\beta H})\)
in any product basis gives a classical input law satisfying full AA
with \(\operatorname{poly}_{D,k,\theta_*}(T,1/e)\) queries,
omitting irrelevant parameters in either case.
The same holds for arbitrary independent binary single-qubit POVMs.
The bound is uniform over the local axes, measurement bases, fields,
and measurement probabilities.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Choose local eigenvectors \(\{|s_i\rangle_i:s_i=\pm1\}\) of
\(N_i\). These product vectors simultaneously diagonalize every
term of \(H\), so
\[
 \frac{e^{-\beta H}}{\operatorname{tr}(e^{-\beta H})}
 =\sum_s\pi(s)\bigotimes_i |s_i\rangle_i\langle s_i|,
\]
where \(\pi\) is exactly the classical Gibbs law in
Theorem~\ref{thm:resolution-channel-closure}. For a product measurement
with local effects \(E_i(x_i)\), the conditional output law factors:
\[
 \Pr[X=x\mid S=s]
 =\prod_i K_i(x_i\mid s_i),\qquad
 K_i(x_i\mid s_i)={}_i\langle s_i|E_i(x_i)|s_i\rangle_i.
\]
Apply that theorem.
\end{proof}

This includes Ising interactions along site-dependent axes observed
in the computational basis. It proves a statement about a measured
quantum Gibbs law even when the Hamiltonian is not diagonal in the
measurement basis. The hypothesis is stronger than commutativity:
all interactions admit a common \emph{product} eigenbasis. General
commuting stabilizer Hamiltonians and transverse-field Ising models
with a genuinely transverse field need not have this structure.
For those models, the local-channel posterior formula (LP) is not
available from this argument.
