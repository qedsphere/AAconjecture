\subsection{Linear query barriers for canonical spectral sampling primitives}
\label{ian:spectral-posterior-barriers}

Status: proved barriers for two specified primitives. In the spectrally
small, delocalized Hadamard family, uniformly sampling one canonical
Hubbard--Stratonovich field coordinate, even to a fixed small total
variation error, requires linearly many input queries. The same holds
for one heat-bath update. These are representation-specific lower
bounds, not a failure of full AA or a universal obstruction to
Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold-local-posterior}.

\begin{lemma}[An adaptive sampler's correlation budget]
\label{ian:adaptive-correlation-budget}
Let \(V_1,\ldots,V_N\) be independent fair spins, put
\[
 S=\sum_jV_j,\qquad Z=S/\sqrt N,
\]
and let an adaptive randomized query procedure output \(B\in\{-1,1\}\).
If \(\tau\) is its number of distinct queried coordinates, then
\[
 |\E[BS]|\le\sqrt{\E\tau}.
 \tag{ian-CB}
\]
Suppose a target output mean is \(m(v)\), and the procedure satisfies
\[
 \left|\E[B\mid V=v]-m(v)\right|\le2\eta
 \qquad\text{for every }v.
\]
Writing \(r=\E[Z\,m(V)]\), it follows that
\[
 \E\tau\ge N(r-2\eta)_+^2.
 \tag{ian-CBL}
\]
Here \(a_+=\max\{a,0\}\). In particular, every worst-case query bound
must be at least this expectation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expose all private randomness, which is independent of \(V\), and
include it in the transcript. Conditional on the final transcript,
the unqueried spins remain independent and fair. Hence
\[
 \E[S\mid\text{transcript}]
 =M:=\sum_{\text{queried }j}V_j.
\]
The revealed-spin sum is a martingale whose successive fresh increments
have conditional mean zero and square one. Since there are at most
\(N\) distinct queries, stopped martingale orthogonality gives
\(\E M^2=\E\tau\). Therefore
\[
 |\E BS|=|\E BM|\le\sqrt{\E M^2}=\sqrt{\E\tau}.
\]
The pointwise approximation assumption and \(\E|S|\le\sqrt N\) give
\[
 \E BS\ge \E[S\,m(V)]-2\eta\E|S|
 \ge\sqrt N(r-2\eta).
\]
Combine this with (ian-CB). Repeated queries can be removed by caching.
\end{proof}

Fix a Walsh Hadamard matrix with first row all \(+1\), and write
\[
 O=H/\sqrt N,\qquad
 R=\begin{pmatrix}0&O\\O^\top&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 K=\lambda R,\qquad 0<\lambda\le1/8.
 \tag{ian-HS-family}
\]
Thus \(\|K\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\lambda\), and every nonzero entry has
magnitude \(\lambda/\sqrt N\).

\begin{proposition}[One canonical Gaussian posterior coordinate costs linearly]
\label{ian:hs-posterior-linear-lower-bound}
Use the positive definite diagonal completion
\[
 A=2\lambda I+K.
\]
In the Hubbard--Stratonovich joint representation of \(\mu_{K,h}\),
\[
 Y\mid X=x\sim\mathcal N(Ax,A).
 \tag{ian-GP}
\]
Suppose an oracle procedure, given \(x=(u,v)\), returns a sample whose
law is within total variation \(\eta\) of the conditional law of
\(Y_{u_1}\), for every input \(x\). If
\[
 \eta\le\frac18\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{\pi}},
\]
then its worst-case spin-query count is at least
\[
 \frac{\lambda N}{4\pi}.
 \tag{ian-GPL}
\]
The same lower bound holds if it is asked only for the posterior bit
\(\mathbf1_{\{Y_{u_1}\ge2\lambda\}}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The eigenvalues of \(A\) are \(\lambda\) and \(3\lambda\), so it is
positive definite. Completing the square in the HS joint density
proves (ian-GP), for every field \(h\).

Restrict attention to inputs with \(u=(1,\ldots,1)\), and give those
fixed spins to the procedure for free. Average the remaining inputs
over independent fair \(V_j\). This averaging law is an adversarial
test of pointwise correctness; it need not be the Gibbs marginal.
By the first row of \(O\),
\[
 Y_{u_1}\mid(u,v)
 \sim\mathcal N\left(
 2\lambda+\lambda\frac{\sum_jv_j}{\sqrt N},\,2\lambda\right).
\]
Consequently the target mean of the sign bit
\(B=2\mathbf1_{\{Y_{u_1}\ge2\lambda\}}-1\) is
\[
 m(v)=2\Phi(bZ)-1,\qquad b=\sqrt{\lambda/2},
\]
where \(\Phi\) is the standard Gaussian distribution function.
Total variation error \(\eta\) changes this bit's mean by at most
\(2\eta\).

For \(t\ge0\),
\[
 2\Phi(t)-1
 =\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\int_0^t e^{-s^2/2}\,ds
 \ge\sqrt{\frac2\pi}\left(t-\frac{t^3}{6}\right).
\]
Oddness therefore gives, for every real \(z\),
\[
 z\bigl(2\Phi(bz)-1\bigr)
 \ge\sqrt{\frac2\pi}
       \left(bz^2-\frac{b^3z^4}{6}\right).
\]
Since \(\E Z^2=1\) and \(\E Z^4=3-2/N\le3\),
\[
 r:=\E[Zm(V)]
 \ge\sqrt{\frac2\pi}b(1-b^2/2)
 =\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{\pi}}(1-\lambda/4).
\]
For the stated range of \(\lambda,\eta\),
\[
 r-2\eta\ge\frac12\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{\pi}}.
\]
Lemma~\ref{ian:adaptive-correlation-budget} now gives
\(\E\tau\ge\lambda N/(4\pi)\). Thresholding a sample of the real
coordinate gives the same bit, so the bound applies to either task.
\end{proof}

This proves more than the observation that the posterior mean depends
on all spins: even one coarse posterior bit has a linear query
requirement at an error threshold depending only on the fixed
temperature. Thus the canonical coordinate oracle cannot satisfy a
dimension-independent \(b(1)\) in the conditional-access requirement
of Proposition~\ref{prop:threshold-local-posterior}.

There is also a separate source-law issue. In this representation,
\(X\mid Y\) is a product law, but the marginal law of \(Y\) is
\[
 \nu(dy)\propto
 e^{-y^\top A^{-1}y/2}\prod_i2\cosh(h_i+y_i)\,dy,
\]
which is not generally a product law. Product conditional spins do not
make the full latent source independent. The lower bound above holds
even if a suitable AA theorem for that latent source were granted.

The conclusion concerns the displayed physical-field coordinates, or
their displayed threshold bits. It does not quantify over all
reparameterizations, encodings, or alternative couplings. In
particular, after pinning all \(u\)'s, the remaining physical Ising law
is itself a product law; one can abandon the original HS representation
and simulate that law directly.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform heat-bath access also costs linearly]
\label{ian:heatbath-linear-lower-bound}
For (ian-HS-family) at zero field, suppose an oracle procedure samples
the heat-bath output at \(u_1\), with total variation error at most
\(\eta\) for every neighbor assignment. If \(\eta\le\lambda/8\), its
worst-case number of spin queries is at least
\[
 \frac{\lambda^2N}{4}.
 \tag{ian-HBL}
\]
More generally, its expected number of distinct queries under fair
independent neighbor spins is at least
\[
 N\bigl(\lambda(1-\lambda^2)-2\eta\bigr)_+^2.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The heat-bath output mean is
\[
 m(v)=\tanh\left(\lambda\frac{\sum_jv_j}{\sqrt N}\right)
      =\tanh(\lambda Z).
\]
For \(t\ge0\),
\[
 \tanh t\ge t-\frac{t^3}{3}.
\]
Indeed, the derivative of
\(\tanh t-t+t^3/3\) is \(t^2-\tanh^2t\ge0\).
Oddness and the fourth-moment calculation above imply
\[
 \E[Z\tanh(\lambda Z)]
 \ge\lambda-\frac{\lambda^3}{3}\E Z^4
 \ge\lambda(1-\lambda^2).
\]
Apply Lemma~\ref{ian:adaptive-correlation-budget}. When
\(\lambda\le1/8\) and \(\eta\le\lambda/8\), the expression inside the
positive part is at least \(\lambda/2\).
\end{proof}

In particular, no exact heat-bath evaluation procedure can have
uniformly bounded expected neighbor-query cost in this family.
An input-independent random update map, evaluated using its selected
parents, is such a procedure. Hence the subcritical expected-parent
estimates used in
\texttt{high\_temperature/classical/05\_arbitrary\_degree.tex},
Theorem~\ref{thm:resolution-dynamics-arbitrary-degree}, cannot follow
from a small spectral norm alone.

This limitation is specific to the elementary update task. For
example, a star with central couplings \(\lambda/\sqrt N\) also has
operator norm \(\lambda\), and its central heat-bath task has exactly
the same lower bound. Nevertheless, the entire star family has
hereditary full AA by
\texttt{simulation/positive\_examples/03\_ising\_forests.tex},
Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold-ising-forests}.
Thus expensive uniform heat-bath access is compatible with a different
successful local-posterior representation. No universal impossibility
statement for forward maps plus posterior access follows from these
barriers.
