\subsection{Recognizable hard sectors and transfer}
\label{ian:hard-sector-transfer}

Status: proved. The Gibbs and product-reference transfers in Proposition~\ref{ian:hard-sector-likelihood-reconciliation} are unconditional, resting on Corollary~\ref{cor:resolution-product-alphabet-aa}, which imports only inequality (BC) of Huffstutler--Miloschewsky--Podder (arXiv:2611.12795). Every other statement, including the counterexample to full-AA transfer under a dimension-uniform KL bound, uses no import.

We use the definitions of \(\ell_\mu\), \(\mathcal D_t\),
\(\mathcal F_t\), and \(\operatorname{Hard}\) from
Definition~\ref{ian:hard-sector-definition}. All trees have worst-case
query depth; preprocessing, computation, and real leaf constants are
unrestricted. Full AA means \eqref{ian:hard-sector-aa-definition}.

\begin{corollary}[Recognizable hard sectors]
\label{ian:recognizable-hard-sectors}
In Theorem~\ref{ian:hard-sector-regularity}, for every \(K\ge1\), the
hard-set alternative can be strengthened to require that \(H\) be a
level set of a finite-valued predictor computed by a deterministic tree
of depth at most \(Kt\); in particular this holds for
\[
 K=\left\lceil\frac1{w\gamma^2}\right\rceil.
\]
Consequently, for every law \(\mu\) on \(\Omega_n\), every
\(Q:\Omega_n\to[0,1]\), integer \(t\ge1\), and
\[
 0<\varepsilon<1/8,\qquad 0<\eta\le1/4,
\]
the assumption
\[
 \ell_\mu\left(
 \left\lceil256\varepsilon^{-7}\eta^{-3}\right\rceil t,Q
 \right)>\varepsilon
\]
yields a set \(H\) satisfying
\[
 \mu(H)\ge\frac{\eta\varepsilon^3}{16},\qquad
 \operatorname{Hard}\left(
 Q,\mu(\cdot\mid H);
 t,\frac{\varepsilon^2}{4},
 \frac{\eta\varepsilon^2}{4}\right),
\]
whose membership is decidable by a deterministic classical tree of
depth at most
\[
 \left\lceil256\varepsilon^{-7}\eta^{-3}\right\rceil t.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{ian:hard-sector-regularity} chooses an
optimal squared-error predictor \(g\) of depth at most \(kt\), for some
\(k<K\), and returns one of its score bins, \(H=\{x:g(x)\in I_j\}\). The
binned predictor \(b(x)=j/M\) for \(g(x)\in I_j\) is a function of \(g\),
hence computed by a tree of depth at most \(kt<Kt\)
(Lemma~\ref{ian:hard-sector-tree-closure}), and \(H=\{b=j/M\}\).

For the explicit conclusion, the proof of
Corollary~\ref{ian:hard-sector-explicit} applies the theorem with
\(a=\varepsilon^2/4\), \(\gamma=\eta a\), \(M=\lceil1/\gamma\rceil\),
\(w=\varepsilon/(2M)\) and
\(K_\dagger=\lceil2/(\varepsilon\gamma^2)\rceil
=\lceil32\varepsilon^{-5}\eta^{-2}\rceil\), and shows
\(w\ge\eta\varepsilon^3/16\). Membership in \(H\),
the indicator \(\mathbf1[b=j/M]\), is a function of \(g\), so it has
depth at most
\(kt<K_\dagger t\le\lceil256\varepsilon^{-7}\eta^{-3}\rceil t\).
\end{proof}

For a Gibbs law
\[
 \mu_\beta(x)=\frac{e^{-\beta E(x)}}{Z(\beta)},\qquad \beta>0,
\]
write
\[
 Z_H(\beta)=\sum_{x\in H}e^{-\beta E(x)},\qquad
 F_H=-\beta^{-1}\log Z_H,\qquad F=-\beta^{-1}\log Z.
\]
The corollary gives a recognizable sector with
\[
 F_H-F
 =\beta^{-1}\log\frac1{\mu_\beta(H)}
 \le\beta^{-1}\log\frac{16}{\eta\varepsilon^3}.
\]
Recognizability is a query statement with unrestricted preprocessing;
its depth can be much larger than the depth \(t\) against which the
sector's acceptance signal is hard.

\begin{proposition}[Bounded-density transfer]
\label{ian:hard-sector-density-transfer}
Let \(\mu,\nu\) be laws on the same finite cube, and suppose
\(\nu\le K\mu\), where \(K\ge1\). For every \(Q:\Omega_n\to[0,1]\)
and integer \(t\ge0\),
\[
 \ell_\nu(t,Q)\le K\ell_\mu(t,Q).
\]
If a family \((\mu_n)\) has full-AA constants \(A,B\ge1\), and
\(\nu_n\le K\mu_n\) for every \(n\), then \((\nu_n)\) has simulation
depth
\[
 \left\lceil A(T+K/\varepsilon)^B\right\rceil
 \le
 \left\lceil AK^B(T+\varepsilon^{-1})^B\right\rceil.
\]
In particular, full AA transfers when \(K\) is bounded by a polynomial
in fixed model parameters alone.

If \(\sigma\) is a smooth hard sector of \(\nu\) of weight \(w\), for
instance the conditional law of a set sector of \(\nu\), then
\(\sigma\) is a smooth hard sector of \(\mu\) of weight \(w/K\) with the
same parameters (it need not be a set sector of \(\mu\)).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For every \(D\in\mathcal D_t\),
\(\E_\nu|D-Q|\le K\E_\mu|D-Q|\); take \(D\) minimizing for \(\mu\).
For full AA, run the \(\mu_n\)-simulator at error \(\varepsilon/K<1\).
Finally, \(\nu\ge w\sigma\) and \(\nu\le K\mu\) give
\(\mu\ge(w/K)\sigma\), and hardness is a property of \(\sigma\) alone.
\end{proof}

A dimension-dependent estimate \(K_n=\operatorname{poly}(n)\) does
not supply the dimension-independent constants required by full AA.
The counterexample in Proposition~\ref{ian:hard-sector-kl-transfer}
already has \(K_n\le\log_2 n\).

\begin{proposition}[Reconciliation with the repo's likelihood criteria]
\label{ian:hard-sector-likelihood-reconciliation}
Let the source law be \(\mu\), the target law be \(\nu\), and put
\(r=d\nu/d\mu\).

If \(p>1\), \(q=p/(p-1)\), and
\(\|r\|_{L^p(\mu)}\le K\), then
\[
 \ell_\nu(t,Q)\le K\ell_\mu(t,Q)^{1/q}.
\]
Thus a source simulation cost \(S(T,1/e)\) transfers to
\[
 S\bigl(T,(K/\varepsilon)^q\bigr).
\]
If instead, uniformly in dimension,
\[
 \E_\mu[r\mathbf1_{\{r>M\}}]\le CM^{-b}
 \qquad(M\ge1)
\]
for fixed \(C,b>0\), it suffices to use source error
\[
 e=\frac{\varepsilon}{2M},\qquad
 M=\max\{1,(2C/\varepsilon)^{1/b}\}.
\]

For a Gibbs law relative to the uniform law \(U_n\), let
\[
 W_n=\beta(\max E_n-\min E_n).
\]
Then
\[
 e^{-W_n}\le\frac{d\mu_{\beta,n}}{dU_n}\le e^{W_n}.
\]
A uniform-input simulator of cost \(S_0(T,1/e)\) consequently gives
acceptance simulation under \(\mu_{\beta,n}\) at cost
\[
 S_0(T,e^{W_n}/\varepsilon).
\]
Corollary~\ref{cor:resolution-product-alphabet-aa} supplies
\(S_0(T,1/e)=O(T^6e^{-15})\), so every \(Q\in\mathcal Q_{n,T}\) and
\(0<\varepsilon<1\) admit a deterministic tree \(D\) of depth
\(O(T^6\exp(15W_n)\varepsilon^{-15})\), with an absolute implied constant,
satisfying \(\E_{\mu_{\beta,n}}|D-Q|\le\varepsilon\). In particular,
\(\sup_nW_n<\infty\) implies full AA. The same conclusion holds with
\(U_n\) replaced by an arbitrary product law \(\pi_n\) on \(\Omega_n\),
for laws \(\mu_n\propto e^{-V_n}\pi_n\), with \(W_n=\max V_n-\min V_n\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For \(A=|D-Q|\in[0,1]\), the inequalities
\[
 \E_\nu A\le K(\E_\mu A)^{1/q},\qquad
 \E_\nu A
 \le M\E_\mu A+\E_\mu[r\mathbf1_{\{r>M\}}]
 \le Me+CM^{-b}\le\varepsilon
\]
are the two estimates in the proof of Proposition~\ref{tht:density-prop}
in \path{foundations/transfers/03_density_transfer.tex}, with source
and target interchanged; the first is H\"older's inequality together
with \(A^q\le A\). Apply them to a minimizing tree for \(\mu\).

For the Gibbs assertion,
\[
 \frac{d\mu_{\beta,n}}{dU_n}(x)
 =\frac{e^{-\beta E_n(x)}}{\E_{U_n}e^{-\beta E_n}},
\]
and bounding the numerator and denominator by their extrema gives
the density bounds. Proposition~\ref{ian:hard-sector-density-transfer}
with \(K=e^{W_n}\) reduces to uniform error
\(\varepsilon e^{-W_n}<1\), and the corollary, applied to the uniform
product law, gives depth at most
\(C_0T^6\exp(15W_n)\varepsilon^{-15}\le C_0\exp(15W_n)(T+\varepsilon^{-1})^{21}\)
for an absolute \(C_0\). For a product reference \(\pi_n\) and
\(\mu_n\propto e^{-V_n}\pi_n\), \(d\mu_n/d\pi_n=e^{-V_n}/\E_{\pi_n}e^{-V_n}\)
and \(\E_{\pi_n}e^{-V_n}\ge e^{-\max V_n}\), so
\(d\mu_n/d\pi_n\le e^{\operatorname{osc}V_n}\); the corollary covers
every product law.
\end{proof}

The Gibbs assertion is the bounded-density (\(p=\infty\)) case,
Proposition~\ref{ian:hard-sector-density-transfer}; the \(L^p\) form of
Proposition~\ref{tht:density-prop} is its analogue for finite \(p\). The bounded-energy subsection
\path{foundations/definitions/05_bounded_energy_sufficient_condition.tex},
under Section~\ref{note:model_definitions}, additionally uses the lower
density bound to transfer influence inequalities. Acceptance simulation
needs only the upper bound, and its cost is polynomial in \(e^{W}\),
not necessarily in \(W\).

\begin{proposition}[KL conversion and its limitation]
\label{ian:hard-sector-kl-transfer}
Let \(\nu\ll\mu\), let \(g\) be real-valued on a finite space, and
let \(s>0\). Then
\[
 \E_\nu g
 \le\frac{D(\nu\|\mu)+\log\E_\mu e^{sg}}s.
\]
For \(0\le g\le1\), \(D(\nu\|\mu)\le C\), and
\(\E_\mu g\le\delta\), this implies
\[
 \E_\nu g
 \le\frac{C+\log(1+\delta(e^s-1))}{s}.
\]
In particular, source error
\[
 \delta\le\exp\left(-\frac{C+\log2}{\varepsilon}\right)
\]
suffices for target error \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\).
Nevertheless, even \(D(\nu_n\|\mu_n)\le1\) for every \(n\) does not
preserve full AA.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the tilted law
\(\mu_s(x)=e^{sg(x)}\mu(x)/\E_\mu e^{sg}\),
\[
 0\le D(\nu\|\mu_s)
 =D(\nu\|\mu)-s\E_\nu g+\log\E_\mu e^{sg}.
\]
For \(g\in[0,1]\), convexity gives
\(e^{sg}\le1+g(e^s-1)\). With
\(s=(C+\log2)/\varepsilon\) the stated \(\delta\) satisfies
\(\delta(e^s-1)\le\delta e^s\le1\), so \(\E_\nu g\le(C+\log2)/s=\varepsilon\).

For the counterexample, let \(m\ge4\) range over even integers,
\(n=2^m\), and use the character code \(C_n\) and the all-plus
codeword \(c_0\) (the character with \(a=0\)) from
Section~\ref{qm:model-and-theorem}. With natural \(\log\), put
\[
 p_m=\frac1m,\qquad q_m=\frac1{\log m},\qquad
 \mu_n=(1-p_m)\delta_{c_0}+p_mU_{C_n},\qquad
 \nu_n=(1-q_m)\delta_{c_0}+q_mU_{C_n}.
\]
At other dimensions use the same point mass for both families.

The source family has full AA unconditionally, even for arbitrary
bounded targets. If \(\varepsilon\ge p_m\), output \(Q(c_0)\).
Otherwise query the \(m\) coordinates indexed by the standard basis
of \(\mathbb F_2^m\). On the support these determine the entire input,
so the output can be exact; and \(m<\varepsilon^{-1}\).

The log-sum inequality, applied to the common two-component mixture,
and \(p_m\le q_m\le1\) give
\[
 D(\nu_n\|\mu_n)
 \le q_m\log\frac{q_m}{p_m}
 +(1-q_m)\log\frac{1-q_m}{1-p_m}
 \le q_m\log\frac1{p_m}=1,
 \qquad
 \frac{d\nu_n}{d\mu_n}\le\frac{q_m}{p_m}
 =\frac{m}{\log m}\le\log_2 n.
\]

For the repo's one-query acceptance function \(P\),
Section~\ref{qm:one-quantum-query-versus-logarithmically-many-classical-queries}
gives, whenever \(t\le m/2-2\),
\[
 \ell_{\nu_n}(t,P)
 \ge q_m\frac{1-2^{t-m/2}}2
 \ge\frac{3q_m}{8}.
\]
Take \(\varepsilon_m=q_m/8\). For every proposed full-AA constants
\(A,B\ge1\), all sufficiently large even \(m\) satisfy
\[
 \left\lceil A(1+8\log m)^B\right\rceil\le m/2-2,
\]
so the proposed depth has loss exceeding \(\varepsilon_m\), and the
target family fails full AA.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[R\'enyi order one is the threshold]
\label{ian:hard-sector-renyi-threshold}
For \(\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty)\) write
\(D_\alpha(\nu\|\mu)=(\alpha-1)^{-1}\log\E_\mu(d\nu/d\mu)^\alpha\), and
\(D_1=D(\nu\|\mu)\), the limit \(\alpha\to1\). For \(\alpha>1\),
\(\|d\nu/d\mu\|_{L^\alpha(\mu)}=e^{(1-1/\alpha)D_\alpha}=:K\). By
Proposition~\ref{tht:density-prop} (with its source and target
interchanged), a dimension-uniform bound on \(D_\alpha(\nu_n\|\mu_n)\)
for one fixed \(\alpha>1\) transfers full AA from \((\mu_n)\) to
\((\nu_n)\): with \(q=\alpha/(\alpha-1)\) and source cost
\(\lceil A(T+y)^B\rceil\), the target cost at error \(\varepsilon\) is at
most \(\lceil AK^{qB}(T+\varepsilon^{-1})^{qB}\rceil\), since
\(T+K^q\varepsilon^{-q}\le K^q(T+\varepsilon^{-1})^q\) for \(T,K\ge1\). In the counterexample above the
codewords other than \(c_0\) carry \(\mu_n\)-mass \(p_m(1-1/n)\) and
density ratio \(q_m/p_m\), so
\[
 \E_{\mu_n}\Bigl(\frac{d\nu_n}{d\mu_n}\Bigr)^\alpha
 \ge\Bigl(1-\frac1n\Bigr)\frac{m^{\alpha-1}}{(\log m)^\alpha}
 \longrightarrow\infty
\]
for every \(\alpha>1\), while \(D_1=D(\nu_n\|\mu_n)\le1\). Thus among
R\'enyi divergences, a uniform bound transfers full AA exactly for
orders \(\alpha>1\); order \(1\), and hence every order
\(\alpha\in(0,1]\) since \(D_\alpha\) is nondecreasing in \(\alpha\),
fails.
\end{remark}
