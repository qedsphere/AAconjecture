\subsection{Admissible component decompositions}
\label{ian:phase-label-admissibility}

Status: proved. Every simulation bound for product or exchangeable components, hence every admissibility assertion that uses the class \(\mathcal C_\cup\) or the polynomial \eqref{ian:phase-label-product-polynomial} with such components, uses the uniform-input simulator \(s_U\) below, which Corollary~\ref{cor:resolution-product-alphabet-aa} supplies; these assertions therefore rest only on inequality (BC) of Huffstutler--Miloschewsky--Podder, the sole import of that corollary. The coordinate-universal certificate, the mass and entropy bounds, and all decomposition identities are unconditional. No influence form of uniform AA is used. Admissibility is a weak condition: among decompositions into components of the named class, it excludes only those whose component min-entropy fails to tend to infinity in probability (Remark~\ref{ian:phase-label-what-admissibility-excludes}).

Write \(N\) for the number of physical spins, \(\Omega_N=\{-1,1\}^N\), and
use \(\mathcal D_t,\mathcal F_t,\ell_\mu(t,Q)\) of
Section~\ref{ian:hard-sector-definitions-forward} and \(\mathcal Q_{N,T}\)
of Theorem~\ref{ian:hard-sector-aa-equivalence}; \(Q\) always denotes an
acceptance function. For a law \(\lambda\) put
\(H_\infty(\lambda)=-\log\max_x\lambda(x)\). Computation and
preprocessing are free. Uniform-input AA enters only in its simulation
form, the hypothesis of
\path{foundations/transfers/01_posterior_access.tex}: every \(T\)-query
acceptance function has a deterministic simulator of mean error at most
\(e\) under the uniform law using \(s_U(T,e)\) queries. We write
\(s_U(T,e)\le C_U(T+e^{-1})^{d_U}\) with fixed \(C_U,d_U\ge1\). This
bound is supplied by Corollary~\ref{cor:resolution-product-alphabet-aa}:
the uniform law is a product law, so \(s_U(T,e)\le CT^6e^{-15}\) with an
absolute constant \(C\), and since
\(T^6e^{-15}\le(T+e^{-1})^{21}\) for \(T\ge1\), \(e\in(0,1)\), one may
take \(C_U=\max\{C,1\}\) and \(d_U=21\). That corollary imports only
inequality (BC) of Huffstutler--Miloschewsky--Podder
(arXiv:2611.12795). Statements below marked ``using \(s_U\)'' rest on
this simulator, hence hold modulo (BC) only. The influence form of
uniform AA
(\path{influence/high_temperature/01_conventions_imported_uniform_theorem.tex},
proved with exponents \((12,12)\) in
Corollary~\ref{ian:product-influence-aa-proved}) is not used in this or
the next two subsections.

\begin{definition}[\(\mathcal C\)-admissible decompositions]
\label{ian:phase-label-certified-admissibility}
Let \(\mathcal C\) be a named class of laws on the cubes \(\Omega_N\)
with a proved common simulation bound: a fixed polynomial \(\Psi\) with
\[
 \forall N,T\ge1\ \ \forall\delta\in(0,1)\ \
 \forall\lambda\in\mathcal C\text{ on }\Omega_N\ \
 \forall Q\in\mathcal Q_{N,T}:\quad
 \ell_\lambda\bigl(\lceil\Psi(T,\delta^{-1})\rceil,Q\bigr)\le\delta.
 \tag{19.1}\label{ian:phase-label-component-bound}
\]
A decomposition of a family \((\mu_N)\) consists of probability spaces
\((\mathcal Z_N,\pi_N)\) and laws \(\mu_{N,z}\), with
\(z\mapsto\mu_{N,z}(x)\) measurable for every \(x\), such that
\(\mu_N=\int\mu_{N,z}\,\pi_N(dz)\). It is fixed before any acceptance
function, accuracy, budget or sector is chosen. It is
\(\mathcal C\)-admissible if every \(\mu_{N,z}\) lies in \(\mathcal C\)
and component min-entropy tends to infinity in probability:
\[
 \pi_N\bigl\{z:H_\infty(\mu_{N,z})<M\bigr\}\longrightarrow0
 \qquad(N\to\infty)\quad\text{for every }M<\infty.
 \tag{19.2}\label{ian:phase-label-anti-atom}
\]
The class \(\mathcal C\) is hereditary if it is closed under pinning
coordinates, deleting deterministic coordinates, and inserting
deterministic coordinates, with the same \(\Psi\).
Condition~\eqref{ian:phase-label-anti-atom} concerns the original
components only; pinning every coordinate produces a point mass.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Measurable choice of component simulators]
\label{ian:phase-label-measurable-choice}
Assume \eqref{ian:phase-label-component-bound} and that every
\(\mu_{N,z}\) lies in \(\mathcal C\). For every \(N,T\ge1\),
\(\delta\in(0,1)\) and \(Q\in\mathcal Q_{N,T}\) there is a measurable map \(z\mapsto D_z\) into
\(\mathcal D_s\), \(s=\lceil\Psi(T,2\delta^{-1})\rceil\), with
\(\E_{\mu_{N,z}}|D_z-Q|\le\delta\) for every \(z\). For a countable label
space with the discrete \(\sigma\)-algebra one may take \(s=\lceil\Psi(T,\delta^{-1})\rceil\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(\mathcal G\) be the finite set of trees in \(\mathcal D_s\) whose
leaf outputs lie in \(\{0,\delta,2\delta,\ldots\}\cap[0,1]\) or equal
\(1\). Rounding the leaves of the tree supplied by
\eqref{ian:phase-label-component-bound} at accuracy \(\delta/2\) moves
each output by at most \(\delta/2\), so some \(D\in\mathcal G\) has
error at most \(\delta\). For fixed \(D\),
\(z\mapsto\sum_x\mu_{N,z}(x)|D(x)-Q(x)|\) is measurable; take the first
such \(D\) in a fixed enumeration of \(\mathcal G\). In the countable
case every choice is measurable.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Exactly what bounded-bias product mixtures cover]
\label{ian:phase-label-bounded-products}
Fix \(0<\alpha<1/2\). The convex hull of product laws whose coordinate
probabilities lie in \([\alpha,1-\alpha]\) is exactly
\[
 \left\{\mathcal L(Y\eta):
     Y\text{ has an arbitrary law on }\Omega_N,\quad
     \Pr(\eta_i=-1)=\alpha\text{ independently of each other and of }Y\right\}.
 \tag{19.3}\label{ian:phase-label-noise-polytope}
\]
Consequently every such mixture satisfies
\[
 \alpha^N\le\mu(x)\le(1-\alpha)^N,
 \qquad
 \mu(\Gamma)\le |\Gamma|(1-\alpha)^N\quad(\Gamma\subseteq\Omega_N).
 \tag{19.4}\label{ian:phase-label-product-mass-bound}
\]
Its components have min-entropy at least \(N\log\frac1{1-\alpha}\) and,
using \(s_U\), the common polynomial
\eqref{ian:phase-label-product-polynomial}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a product component write \(\Pr(x_i=1)=\alpha+(1-2\alpha)t_i\) with
\(t_i\in[0,1]\). Independent latent signs \(Y_i\) with
\(\Pr(Y_i=1)=t_i\), followed by the independent crossover-\(\alpha\)
channel, generate the component; mixing gives one inclusion in
\eqref{ian:phase-label-noise-polytope}, and conditioning on \(Y\) gives
the other. The atom bounds hold coordinatewise. The simulation claim is
Proposition~\ref{ian:phase-label-union-class}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The bias bound is substantive]
\label{ian:phase-label-bias-bound-remark}
Equivalently to \eqref{ian:phase-label-noise-polytope}, the inverse noise
operator, which multiplies the Fourier coefficient on \(S\) by
\((1-2\alpha)^{-|S|}\), must map \(\mu\) to a nonnegative vector. The
atom lower bound alone does not suffice: for
\((1-2\alpha)^2<c<1-2\alpha\), the law \(\mu(x_1,x_2)=(1+cx_1x_2)/4\)
has one-site conditional probabilities \((1\pm c)/2\in[\alpha,1-\alpha]\)
and atoms at least \((1-c)/4>\alpha/2\ge\alpha^2\), but
\eqref{ian:phase-label-noise-polytope} would force
\(|\E X_1X_2|\le(1-2\alpha)^2<c\). Conversely, at fixed \(N\) the inverse
noise operator tends to the identity as \(\alpha\downarrow0\), so every
strictly positive law has representation
\eqref{ian:phase-label-noise-polytope} for some small enough
\(\alpha=\alpha_N\). A fixed \(\alpha\), or a separate anti-atom
condition, is therefore what carries content.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[An incompatibility with the exact code obstruction]
\label{ian:phase-label-code-entropy-limitation}
Let \(n=2^m\) and let \(C_n=\{c_a:a\in\mathbb F_2^m\}\) be the character
code. If every component of a decomposition of \(\mu_n\) has min-entropy
at least \(cn\), then \(\mu_n(C_n)\le ne^{-cn}\). In particular no
family with \(\mu_n(C_n)\) bounded below by a positive constant, such as \(U_{C_n}\) or the
character-code Gibbs law at \(\beta\ge1\) (code mass at least \(1/2\),
Proposition~\ref{ian:hard-sector-character-instance}), has a fixed-\(\alpha\)
bounded-bias product decomposition, or any decomposition with
min-entropy linear in \(n\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\(\mu_n(C_n)=\int\mu_{n,z}(C_n)\,\pi_n(dz)\le|C_n|e^{-cn}\); for
bounded-bias products use \eqref{ian:phase-label-product-mass-bound}.
\end{proof}

The literal decomposition of \(U_{C_n}\) by the character label \(A\) has
singleton components and violates \eqref{ian:phase-label-anti-atom}.
Here is a non-atomic alternative.

\begin{definition}[Coordinate-universal supports]
\label{ian:phase-label-coordinate-universal}
A set \(\mathcal S\subseteq\Omega_N\) is coordinate-universal if every
Boolean function on \(\mathcal S\) is constant or equals
\((1+\sigma x_i)/2\) on \(\mathcal S\) for some coordinate \(i\) and sign
\(\sigma\). Let \(\mathcal U\) consist of all laws supported on such
sets.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Simulation on coordinate-universal supports]
\label{ian:phase-label-universal-support-simulation}
A coordinate-universal set satisfies \(|\mathcal S|\le\log_2(2N+2)\).
Every \(Q:\mathcal S\to[0,1]\), with no quantum restriction, has a
deterministic approximation of pointwise error at most \(\delta\) using
at most \(\max\{1,\lceil\log_2(1/\delta)\rceil\}\) queries. The class
\(\mathcal U\) is hereditary.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The \(2^{|\mathcal S|}\) Boolean functions on \(\mathcal S\) are among
the \(2N+2\) functions \(0,1,(1\pm x_i)/2\).

Truncate the binary expansion of \(Q\) to the indicated number of bits,
replacing the value one by \(1-2^{-k}\). Each bit is a Boolean function
on \(\mathcal S\), hence constant or one signed coordinate; query these
coordinates and reconstruct.

After pinning, the support is a subset \(\mathcal S'\subseteq\mathcal S\).
Extend a Boolean function on \(\mathcal S'\) arbitrarily to
\(\mathcal S\); the coordinate realizing the extension realizes the
function on \(\mathcal S'\). A deterministic coordinate realizes only
constants, so deleting it loses no nonconstant function, and inserting
one changes nothing.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[An exact \(\mathcal U\)-decomposition of character orbits]
\label{ian:phase-label-simplex-decomposition}
Let \(E_m=\{0,e_1,\ldots,e_m\}\subseteq\mathbb F_2^m\). Let \(\xi\) be
uniform on \(\mathbb F_2^m\), let \(\kappa\) be independently uniform on
\(\{0,\ldots,m\}\), and set
\[
 A=\xi+e_\kappa,\qquad e_0=0.
 \tag{19.5}\label{ian:phase-label-simplex}
\]
Then \(A\) is uniform, and conditional on \(\xi\) the configuration
\(c_A\) is uniform on a coordinate-universal set of size \(m+1\).

The same holds for physical inputs of the form
\[
 X_{\rm vis}=\sigma(W)c_A\eta(W),\qquad
 X_{\rm side}=f_W(A),
 \tag{19.6}\label{ian:phase-label-orbit-input}
\]
with \(A\) uniform and independent of the side information \(W\): realize
\(A\) by \eqref{ian:phase-label-simplex} with \((\xi,\kappa)\) independent
of \(W\) and use the label \(Z=(W,\xi)\). Every component has
min-entropy \(\log(m+1)\) and the simulation bound of
Lemma~\ref{ian:phase-label-universal-support-simulation}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Translation by \(e_\kappa\) preserves the uniform law. Given Boolean
values \(b_0,\ldots,b_m\), choose \(z\in\mathbb F_2^m\) with
\(z_i=b_i+b_0\); then \((-1)^{(\xi+e_j)\cdot z}=(-1)^{\xi\cdot z}(-1)^{z_j}\)
(\(z_0:=0\)) realizes them up to a fixed output sign, and fixed signs
\(\sigma\eta_z\) are absorbed into that sign. The visible configurations
for distinct \(j\) are distinct, so side coordinates change neither the
atom sizes nor universality.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Global character randomization]
\label{ian:phase-label-character-randomization}
Let \(n=2^m\), and let \((X,R,\Theta)\) be random with \(X\in\Omega_n\),
\(R\) finite-valued, \(\Theta\) independent of \((X,R)\), and the law of
\((X,R)\) invariant under \((x,\rho)\mapsto(xc_b,\rho)\) for every
\(b\in\mathbb F_2^m\) (coordinatewise product). Let \(A\) be uniform and
independent of \((X,R,\Theta)\), and put \(\eta=c_AX\). Then \(X=c_A\eta\),
\((\eta,R)\) has the law of \((X,R)\), and \(A\) is independent of
\(W=(\eta,R,\Theta)\). Hence any further spins \(S=f(X,R,\Theta)\) give a
representation \eqref{ian:phase-label-orbit-input} of the law of
\((X,R,S)\), with \(\sigma\equiv1\) and \(X_{\rm side}=(R,f(c_A\eta,R,\Theta))\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\(\Pr(A=a,\eta=y,R=\rho)=2^{-m}\Pr(X=c_ay,R=\rho)=2^{-m}\Pr(X=y,R=\rho)\),
and \(\Theta\) is independent of \((A,X,R)\). Finally \(c_A^2=1\).
\end{proof}

Every conditional law of finitely many spins given \((X,R)\) can be
realized as \(f(X,R,\Theta)\) with \(\Theta\) uniform on \([0,1]\), so the
lemma applies to every law whose \((X,R)\)-marginal is character
invariant. This yields \(\mathcal U\)-decompositions on the whole cube,
not only on a good event.

\begin{proposition}[The union class]
\label{ian:phase-label-union-class}
Let \(\mathcal C_\cup\) consist of
(i) product laws with arbitrary coordinate probabilities, deterministic
coordinates allowed;
(ii) the class \(\mathcal U\);
(iii) laws that are exchangeable after deletion of their deterministic
coordinates.
Then \(\mathcal C_\cup\) is hereditary. Part (ii) satisfies
\eqref{ian:phase-label-component-bound} unconditionally with
\(\Psi_{\mathcal U}(T,y)=1+y\). Using \(s_U\), part (i)
satisfies it with
\[
 \Psi_{\rm prod}(T,y)=(C_U14^{d_U}+2)(T+y)^{2d_U},
 \tag{19.7}\label{ian:phase-label-product-polynomial}
\]
part (iii) with a polynomial majorant \(\Psi_{\rm exch}\) of the bound in
\ref{exch:heading-2}, and \(\mathcal C_\cup\) with
\(\Psi_\cup=\Psi_{\rm prod}+\Psi_{\mathcal U}+\Psi_{\rm exch}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Pinning a product gives a product. Conditioning an exchangeable law on
the values of a fixed set of coordinates leaves it invariant under
permutations of the remaining coordinates. Deletion and insertion of
deterministic coordinates are immediate in (i) and (iii), and (ii) is
Lemma~\ref{ian:phase-label-universal-support-simulation}. The simulator
may depend on the component, so queries to deterministic coordinates
are answered without querying; the remaining acceptance function is a
\(T\)-query function of the other coordinates.

For (i), Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold-block-products} in
\path{simulation/positive_examples/02_arbitrary_blocks.tex}, with block
size one and \(e=\delta\), uses \(s_U(2bT,\delta/2)\) queries, where
\(b=1+\lceil\log_2(64T^2\delta^{-2})\rceil\). With \(x=T+\delta^{-1}\ge2\),
\(b\le4+4\log_2x\le6x\), so
\(s_U(2bT,\delta/2)\le C_U(12xT+2\delta^{-1})^{d_U}\le C_U14^{d_U}x^{2d_U}\).
For (iii) use \ref{exch:heading-2} in
\path{simulation/exchangeable_inputs/01_theorem.tex}, whose hypothesis
is the bound on \(s_U\) and whose single polynomial covers every
exchangeable law. For (ii) note
\(\max\{1,\lceil\log_2\delta^{-1}\rceil\}\le1+\delta^{-1}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What admissibility excludes]
\label{ian:phase-label-what-admissibility-excludes}
First, \eqref{ian:phase-label-anti-atom} holds if and only if there are
\(h_N\to\infty\) and \(b_N\to0\) with
\(\pi_N\{z:H_\infty(\mu_{N,z})<h_N\}\le b_N\). One direction is
immediate. Conversely choose strictly increasing \(N_k\) with
\(\pi_N\{H_\infty<k\}\le1/k\) for \(N\ge N_k\), and put \(h_N=k\),
\(b_N=1/k\) for \(N_k\le N<N_{k+1}\) (and \(h_N=0\), \(b_N=1\) for
\(N<N_1\)). So the definition excludes \(Z=X\)
(point-mass components) and, more generally, exactly the decompositions
along which component min-entropy fails to tend to infinity in
probability; apart from membership of the components in
\(\mathcal C\), it excludes nothing more.

Second, \(\mathcal U\)-components are tiny: by
Lemma~\ref{ian:phase-label-universal-support-simulation} their
min-entropy is at most \(\log\log_2(2N+2)\), and they simulate every
bounded target, quantum or not. They satisfy
\eqref{ian:phase-label-anti-atom} as soon as their maximal atoms tend to
zero in probability, for example for uniform components with growing
support, as in Proposition~\ref{ian:phase-label-simplex-decomposition}.

Third, the label may be the entire relevant input. For arbitrary laws
\(\nu_n\) on \(\Omega_n\), the law \(\nu_n\otimes U_n\) on \(\Omega_{2n}\)
has the padding decomposition with label \(y\sim\nu_n\) and components
\(\mathbf 1_{\{y\}}\otimes U_n\). These are products with min-entropy
\(n\log2\), so, using \(s_U\), the padding decomposition is
\(\mathcal C_\cup\)-admissible. The independent-set pinning
decomposition (item 5 below), whose label is every spin outside an
independent set, is \(\mathcal C_\cup\)-admissible in the same way.

Thus \(\mathcal C_\cup\)-admissibility is a non-degeneracy condition on
component size, not a restriction on what the label encodes, and it does
not single out a physical phase.
\end{remark}

There is also a nonhereditary certificate.

\begin{lemma}[Exponentially close product components]
\label{ian:phase-label-close-products}
Suppose \(\|\mu_{N,z}-p_{N,z}\|_{\rm TV}\le Ce^{-cN}\) uniformly in
\(z,N\), where \(p_{N,z}\) are product laws and \(C,c>0\) are fixed.
Using \(s_U\), the \(\mu_{N,z}\) have a common simulation
polynomial. If the products have coordinate probabilities in a fixed
\([\alpha,1-\alpha]\), then \(H_\infty(\mu_{N,z})\ge c'N\) for some
\(c'>0\) and all large \(N\). No closure under pinning is asserted.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(Ce^{-cN}\le\delta/2\), simulate \(p_{N,z}\) at error \(\delta/2\);
since \(|D-Q|\in[0,1]\) the error on \(\mu_{N,z}\) is at most \(\delta\).
Otherwise \(N<c^{-1}\log(2C/\delta)\), and reading the whole input costs
at most \(c^{-1}(\log(2C)+\delta^{-1})\). The maximum atom is at most
\((1-\alpha)^N+Ce^{-cN}\).
\end{proof}

The repo instances have the following decompositions. Throughout,
\(n=2^m\), \(c_a(z)=(-1)^{a\cdot z}\), and the signed characters are
\(\pm c_a\).

\begin{enumerate}
\item The character-code mixture \((1-w_n)U_n+w_nU_{C_n}\): one product
component \(U_n\) and the decomposition
\eqref{ian:phase-label-simplex} of \(U_{C_n}\). It is
\(\mathcal C_\cup\)-admissible, using \(s_U\) for the product
component. By \eqref{ian:phase-label-product-mass-bound} and
\(\mu_n(C_n)\ge w_n\), no decomposition into products with probabilities
in \([\alpha,1-\alpha]\) exists at any index with \(w_n>n(1-\alpha)^n\).
Hence there is no fixed-\(\alpha\) such decomposition of the family if,
for every \(\alpha\in(0,1/2)\), \(w_n>n(1-\alpha)^n\) for infinitely many
\(n\).

\item The noisy code \(X=(-1)^Bc_A\eta\) with \(\E\eta_i=\rho\in(0,1)\),
the law \(\nu_{n,\rho}\) of Theorem~\ref{threshold:noisy-query-theorem}:
the natural label \((A,B)\) gives products with probabilities in
\([\alpha,1-\alpha]\), \(\alpha=(1-\rho)/2\), hence a
\(\mathcal C_\cup\)-admissible decomposition, using \(s_U\).

\item The soft code \(\mu\propto S_L^\beta\) of
Proposition~\ref{ian:hard-sector-soft-instance}. Put \(\rho=\tanh(\beta L)\),
\(q=\nu_{n,\rho}=(2n)^{-1}\sum_vq_v\), where \(q_v\) is the product law
with \(\E_{q_v}X_z=\rho v_z\), and \(\omega=\mu/q\) (the ratio
\(R/\E_qR\) in the proof of that proposition). The maps \(x\mapsto-x\)
and \(x\mapsto xc_b\) generate a group acting transitively on signed
characters and preserving \(\mu\), \(q\) and \(\omega\). Hence
\[
 \Pr(V=v,X=x)=\frac{q_v(x)\,\omega(x)}{2n}
 \tag{19.8}\label{ian:phase-label-soft-joint}
\]
is a joint law with \(X\)-marginal \(\mu\) and uniform \(V\), because
\(\E_{q_v}\omega\) is the same for all \(v\) and averages to
\(\E_q\omega=1\). Its components \(\lambda_v=q_v\omega\) satisfy
\(\|\lambda_v-q_v\|_{\rm TV}=\frac12\E_{q_v}|\omega-1|\), again
independent of \(v\) and so equal to
\(\frac12\E_q|\omega-1|=\|\mu-q\|_{\rm TV}\le\tau_n\), with \(\tau_n\)
exponentially small at fixed \(\beta,L\) (proof of
Proposition~\ref{ian:hard-sector-soft-instance}, from
Theorem~\ref{threshold:soft-code-theorem}). Lemma~\ref{ian:phase-label-close-products}
applies with \(\alpha=(1-\rho)/2\), using \(s_U\); hereditary
simulation of the \(\lambda_v\) is not established.
Since \(\mu\) is character invariant,
Lemma~\ref{ian:phase-label-character-randomization} (with \(R,\Theta\)
trivial) and Proposition~\ref{ian:phase-label-simplex-decomposition} give
a hereditary \(\mathcal U\)-decomposition, unconditionally. At positive
integer \(\beta\), expanding \(S_L^\beta\) gives an exact product
decomposition with label \((v_1,\ldots,v_\beta)\), fields
\(L\sum_{j=1}^\beta v_j(z)\), and minimum probability
\((1+e^{2\beta L})^{-1}\).

\item The local families of
Proposition~\ref{ian:hard-sector-local-instances}. The dense and sparse
three-local laws are character invariant, since every test
\(x_zx_wx_{z+w}\) is. In the pairwise model the visible marginal is a
function of the same tests (the display after that proposition), and in
the zero-field model the \((X,R)\)-marginal, \(R\) the reference spins,
is invariant under \(X\mapsto Xc_b\) with \(R\) fixed (its proof).
Lemma~\ref{ian:phase-label-character-randomization}, with the auxiliary
spins realized from an independent seed \(\Theta\), and
Proposition~\ref{ian:phase-label-simplex-decomposition} give
\(\mathcal U\)-decompositions of the full physical law, unconditionally.

\item Independent-set pinning, for local families of bounded degree
\(d\) in the primal graph of the interaction hypergraph (two spins
adjacent when they share a term), such as the sparse three-local,
pairwise and zero-field families. Let \(J_*\) bound the total incident
absolute strength of each spin, fields included, as in the repo. Choose
an independent set \(I\) (greedily, \(|I|\ge N/(d+1)\) is possible) and
the label \(Z=X_{I^c}\). No term contains two spins of \(I\), so given \(Z\) the
spins of \(I\) are independent with conditional fields of magnitude at
most \(J_*\), hence probabilities in
\([\alpha_\beta,1-\alpha_\beta]\), \(\alpha_\beta=(1+e^{2\beta J_*})^{-1}\).
The components are products, deterministic on \(I^c\), with
\(H_\infty\ge|I|\log\frac1{1-\alpha_\beta}\); the decomposition is
\(\mathcal C_\cup\)-admissible, using \(s_U\).
\end{enumerate}

Thus each instance listed above has a \(\mathcal C_\cup\)-admissible
decomposition, using \(s_U\), hence modulo (BC) only, wherever product
or exchangeable components occur. This does not extend to every repo
obstruction: since \(\mu(x)\le\pi\{H_\infty<M\}+e^{-M}\) for every
decomposition, a law with an atom of mass bounded below admits no
decomposition satisfying \eqref{ian:phase-label-anti-atom}; the
nonmonotone family of Section~\ref{sec:resolution-nonmonotone} at
\(\beta=2\) has \(\mu(c_0)=1/Z_n\to1/2\). By Remark~\ref{ian:phase-label-what-admissibility-excludes}
this reflects the weakness of admissibility, not physical phase
structure; the next subsection keeps component labels, component
programs, and phases apart.
