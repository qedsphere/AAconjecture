\subsection{Sampling, Glauber mixing, and acceptance simulation}
\label{ian:hard-sector-sampling}

Status: proved in the stated computation models; the Curie--Weiss full-AA assertion is conditional on uniform-input AA, and its mixing estimate is imported from literature.

A uniform exact sampler here is a probabilistic Turing machine using
independent fair bits, halting almost surely, whose output has exactly
the specified law and whose expected running time is polynomial in
the number of output spins. A fixed real parameter \(\beta\) is
efficiently computable if certified dyadic approximations with error
at most \(2^{-b}\) can be computed in time polynomial in \(b\).
An approximate sampler has deterministic polynomial running time
in \(n,\log(1/\delta)\) and output TV error at most \(\delta\).

\begin{theorem}[Efficient exact sampling of the character-code Gibbs law]
\label{ian:hard-sector-character-sampler}
For every fixed efficiently computable finite \(\beta\ge1\), the
character-code Gibbs family of Section~\ref{qm:model-and-theorem}, extended by the uniform law at dimensions not of the form \(2^m\) with \(m\) even, has a uniform efficient exact
sampler. It also has an approximate sampler with running time
\(\operatorname{poly}_\beta(n,\log(1/\delta))\).
Nevertheless, it fails full AA at every such \(\beta\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For \(n=2^m\), \(m\) even, use the exact identity
\[
 \mu_{\beta,n}=(1-w_{\beta,n})U_n+w_{\beta,n}U_{C_n},
\]
where
\[
 \delta_n=n2^{-n},\qquad
 \Delta_n=\log(1+2^n/n),\qquad
 w_{\beta,n}
 =\frac{\delta_n(e^{\beta\Delta_n}-1)}
        {1+\delta_n(e^{\beta\Delta_n}-1)}.
\]
This is Section~\ref{qm:exact-mixture-identity-and-transition-width};
in particular \(w_{1,n}=1/2\) exactly.

First sample a Bernoulli variable with parameter \(w_{\beta,n}\).
On outcome zero output \(n\) independent fair signs. On outcome one,
sample \(A\) using \(m\) fair bits and output
\[
 (c_A)_z=(-1)^{A\cdot z}\qquad(z\in\mathbb F_2^m).
\]
The latter computation takes \(O(nm)\) elementary bit operations.

For completeness, the Bernoulli step can be exact despite an
irrational weight. Elementary certified arithmetic computes an
approximation to \(w_{\beta,n}\) with error \(2^{-b}\) in time
\(\operatorname{poly}_\beta(n+b)\). Indeed, the integers \(2^n,n\)
have \(O(n)\) bits, \(\Delta_n=O(n)\), and a fixed-\(\beta\)
exponential has \(O_\beta(n)\) integer bits. Polynomial-time
precision access to \(\beta\), together with range reduction for
logarithms and exponentials, suffices.

Generate the binary expansion of a uniform \(U\in[0,1]\) one bit at
a time. At stage \(b\), its known prefix locates it in an interval
of length \(2^{-b}\). Compute a certified interval for the weight
with radius \(2^{-(b+2)}\). Stop once the two intervals are disjoint,
and return \(\mathbf1_{\{U<w_{\beta,n}\}}\).
If \(B\) is the stopping stage, failure to stop by stage \(b\)
implies that \(U\) lies within \(2\cdot2^{-b}\) of the true weight.
Thus
\[
 \Pr(B>b)\le\min\{1,4\cdot2^{-b}\}.
\]
The algorithm halts almost surely and returns an exact Bernoulli
sample. Since each stage costs at most a fixed polynomial in \(n+b\),
\[
 \E[\text{running time}]
 \le\sum_{b\ge1}\Pr(B\ge b)\,
       \operatorname{poly}_\beta(n+b)
 =\operatorname{poly}_\beta(n).
\]

For a bounded-time approximation, truncate after
\[
 b_*=\left\lceil\log_2(8/\delta)\right\rceil
\]
stages and use any fixed branch if unresolved. Coupling to the exact
procedure bounds TV error by
\(4\cdot2^{-b_*}\le\delta/2\).
All remaining operations are exact and have polynomial cost.

Finally,
Section~\ref{qm:one-quantum-query-versus-logarithmically-many-classical-queries}
proves failure of full AA for this same law at every fixed
\(\beta\ge1\). The sampler does not determine the hidden label of an
unknown input supplied to the query algorithm.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Curie--Weiss: full AA with exponentially slow Glauber mixing]
\label{ian:hard-sector-curie-weiss-sampling}
Assume uniform-input AA. For every fixed \(\beta>1\), the laws
\[
 \pi_{\beta,n}(x)
 \propto\exp\left(\frac{\beta}{2n}
                    \left(\sum_i x_i\right)^2\right)
\]
have full AA, while discrete-time random-site heat-bath Glauber
dynamics satisfies
\[
 t_{\rm mix}(1/4)\ge e^{c_\beta n}
 \qquad(n\ge n_0(\beta))
\]
for some \(c_\beta>0\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
These laws are permutation invariant, so the full-AA assertion is
Section~\ref{exch:heading-2} in
\path{simulation/exchangeable_inputs/01_theorem.tex}, with precisely
its uniform-input AA assumption.

The mixing assertion is an external import from
Ding--Lubetzky--Peres, Theorem 3, for ordinary random-site heat-bath
dynamics. Their normalization is
\[
 \exp\left(\frac{\beta}{n}\sum_{i<j}x_ix_j\right),
\]
which differs from ours by a constant factor in the unnormalized
weight, since \((\sum_i x_i)^2=n+2\sum_{i<j}x_ix_j\).
Their update rule (uniform random site, heat-bath resampling) and worst-initial-state TV mixing convention agree with ours. Theorem 3 assumes only \(\delta^2n\to\infty\), which holds for fixed \(\delta=\beta-1>0\); since \(g(x)=\tanh(\beta x-\operatorname{artanh}x)\), its exponent \(\tfrac12\int_0^\zeta\log\frac{1+g}{1-g}\,dx\) equals \(I_\beta\), with \(\zeta=m_\beta\).
% Literature source: [Ding--Lubetzky--Peres, Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.1906).

More explicitly, let \(m_\beta\in(0,1)\) solve
\(m_\beta=\tanh(\beta m_\beta)\), and put
\[
 I_\beta=\int_0^{m_\beta}
             \bigl(\beta x-\operatorname{artanh}x\bigr)\,dx>0.
\]
The imported theorem gives
\(t_{\rm mix}(1/4)=\Theta_\beta(n e^{nI_\beta})\).
It therefore permits the explicit exponent
\(c_\beta=I_\beta/2\), after increasing \(n_0(\beta)\).
No mixing assertion is being deduced from the repo's simulation
theorem.
\end{proof}

The preceding two examples establish that efficient sampling is not
sufficient, and polynomial Glauber mixing is not necessary, for full
AA. To obtain both directions for both notions, two additional
arguments are required.

\begin{proposition}[Polynomial Glauber mixing is not sufficient]
\label{ian:hard-sector-padded-fast-mixing}
There is an efficiently exactly samplable family with polynomial
random-site heat-bath mixing time that fails full AA.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix \(\beta=1\). Let \(d=2^m\), with \(m\) even, and put \(N=2^d\).
On \(N\) spins define
\[
 \Pi_N=\mu_{1,d}\otimes U_{N-d},
\]
where the first factor is the character-code law on \(d\) spins.
At other dimensions use the uniform law. These dimensions and the
code block are computably recognizable. The preceding sampler,
followed by independent fair padding, runs in expected
\(\operatorname{poly}(N)\) time.

The one-query witness on the code block still requires
\(\Omega(\log d)\) classical queries at error \(1/8\).
Indeed, fixing every padding spin reduces any classical tree to a
tree on the code block of no larger depth; average the repo lower
bound over the independent padding. Since \(d\to\infty\), no fixed
full-AA polynomial at \(T=1,\varepsilon=1/8\) can suffice.

We verify mixing rather than inferring it from samplability.
For any Gibbs law \(\lambda\) on \(N\) spins with dimensionless
energy oscillation \(W\), let \(P_\lambda\) be random-site heat-bath
dynamics. Relative to \(U_N\), write its minimum and maximum density
as \(r_{\min},r_{\max}\); then
\[
 \frac{r_{\max}}{r_{\min}}=e^W.
\]
For neighboring configurations \(x,y\), its stationary edge flow is
\[
 \lambda(x)P_\lambda(x,y)
 =\frac1N\frac{\lambda(x)\lambda(y)}{\lambda(x)+\lambda(y)}
 \ge r_{\min}\frac{U_N(x)}{2N}.
\]
Its Dirichlet form therefore dominates \(r_{\min}\) times the uniform
heat-bath Dirichlet form, while
\[
 \Var_\lambda f\le r_{\max}\Var_{U_N}f.
\]
Uniform heat-bath has spectral gap \(1/N\), hence
\[
 \operatorname{gap}(P_\lambda)\ge\frac{e^{-W}}N.
\]
Heat-bath is an average of conditional-expectation projections, so
its spectrum is nonnegative. The elementary \(L^2\)-to-TV bound,
together with \(\lambda_{\min}\ge e^{-W}2^{-N}\), gives
\[
 t_{\rm mix}(1/4)
 \le\left\lceil
 Ne^W\bigl(N\log2+W+\log4\bigr)\right\rceil.
\]

For \(\Pi_N\),
\[
 W=\Delta_d=\log(1+2^d/d)\le d\log2=\log N.
\]
Consequently
\[
 t_{\rm mix}(1/4)
 \le\left\lceil
 N^2\bigl(N\log2+\log N+\log4\bigr)\right\rceil.
\]
Thus ordinary single-spin Glauber mixing is polynomial in the
physical dimension \(N\), despite failure of full AA. Its transition
probabilities also have polynomial-time precision access.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Efficient samplability is not necessary in the unrestricted query model]
\label{ian:hard-sector-sampling-not-necessary}
There is a uniformly computable full-support family of laws with full
AA that has no uniform exact sampler of expected polynomial running
time.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Enumerate triples \((M,c,k)\), where \(M\) is a fair-bit probabilistic
Turing machine and \(c,k\) are positive integers, repeating every
triple infinitely often. Associate the \(n\)-th triple to length \(n\).
Set
\[
 L_n=\left\lceil8cn^k\right\rceil.
\]
By exhaustive enumeration of all random bit strings of length \(L_n\),
compute the probability that \(M(1^n)\) has completed an \(n\)-bit
output by time \(L_n\) with first bit \(b\), for \(b=0,1\).
Their sum is at most one. Choose \(b_n\) with this probability at most
\(1/2\), breaking ties deterministically, and set
\(z_n=b_n0^{n-1}\). This defines a computable sequence.

Now define, identifying bits with signs,
\[
 \lambda_n=(1-2^{-n})\delta_{z_n}+2^{-n}U_n.
\]
Every atom has positive computable probability.

For every \(Q:\Omega_n\to[0,1]\), if
\(\varepsilon\ge2^{-n}\), output the constant \(Q(z_n)\).
Otherwise read the entire input and output \(Q\) exactly. In the
second case \(n<\log_2(1/\varepsilon)\). Thus this family has full AA,
even for arbitrary bounded targets, with unrestricted preprocessing
as stipulated in the query model.

Suppose \(M\) exactly sampled \(\lambda_n\) with expected running time
at most \(cn^k\) for all sufficiently large \(n\). A sufficiently late
occurrence of its triple has that property. Markov's inequality gives
\(\Pr(\text{running time}>L_n)\le1/8\). Exact sampling requires
\[
 \Pr(\text{first output bit}=b_n)
 =1-2^{-n-1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
 \Pr(\text{completed by }L_n
       \text{ with first bit }b_n)
 \ge\frac78-2^{-n-1}>\frac12,
\]
contradicting the definition of \(b_n\).

This argument excludes every expected-polynomial uniform exact
sampler. The family can also be written as a computable Gibbs law
at \(\beta=1\), using \(E_n(x)=-\log\lambda_n(x)\).
No polynomial-time energy-evaluation promise is asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The logical separation]
\label{ian:hard-sector-sampling-separation}
In the repo's unrestricted query model, and assuming uniform-input
AA for the Curie--Weiss implication, neither efficient exact
samplability nor polynomial random-site heat-bath Glauber mixing is
necessary or sufficient for full AA.

Efficient samplability is not sufficient by
Theorem~\ref{ian:hard-sector-character-sampler}, and is not necessary
by Proposition~\ref{ian:hard-sector-sampling-not-necessary}.
Polynomial Glauber mixing is not sufficient by
Proposition~\ref{ian:hard-sector-padded-fast-mixing}, and is not
necessary by Proposition~\ref{ian:hard-sector-curie-weiss-sampling}.
Only the last implication uses uniform-input AA.

If one additionally restricts to polynomial-time evaluable Gibbs
Hamiltonians with polynomial-time precision access, the diagonal
non-samplability construction does not establish necessity failure
inside that narrower class. The other three separations above retain
their stated effective models.
\end{corollary}
