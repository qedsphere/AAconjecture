\subsection{Random sets, products, and failure of necessity}
\label{ian:gamma-set-products-and-necessity}

Status: proved. The failures of \(\Gamma^{\mathrm{rand}}\) and \(\Gamma^{\mathrm{set}}\) (Propositions~\ref{ian:triple-random-set-failure} and~\ref{ian:gamma-set-not-necessary}) and the product implication (Proposition~\ref{ian:product-global-to-local}) are unconditional. The hereditary full-AA assertions use \path{foundations/product_alphabets.tex}, Corollary~\ref{cor:resolution-product-alphabet-aa}, whose only external import is its counting inequality (BC); neither the influence form (UAA) nor a uniform-input simulator is used in this subsection.

Notation \(D_i\), \(I_i^\mu\), the reveal gain \(G_\mu(f,S)\), and the constants of \(\Gamma^{\mathrm{rand}},\Gamma^{\mathrm{set}}\) are those of Subsection~\ref{ian:generalized-influence-bridge}. For a class \(\mathcal A\) of laws, \(\operatorname{Pin}\mathcal A\) denotes its pinning closure: all residual laws obtained from members of \(\mathcal A\) by positive-probability pinnings, including the empty pinning.

\begin{proposition}[Random sets are not necessary]
\label{ian:triple-random-set-failure}
Let \(\mu_m\) be the product of \(m\) independent blocks, each uniform
on \(\{(+,+,+),(-,-,-)\}\), and put
\[
 f(x)=M_3(x_1,x_2,x_3)
 :=\frac{x_1+x_2+x_3-x_1x_2x_3}{2}.
\]
This witness defeats \(\Gamma^{\mathrm{rand}}\) for every proposed
choice of constants. Nevertheless, it has a singleton revealing all
its variance, and the pinning closure of \(\{\mu_m:m\ge1\}\) has
hereditary full AA.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The polynomial \(M_3\) is Boolean on the entire cube and has degree
three. On the support of \(\mu_m\),
\[
 f=X_1=X_2=X_3,\qquad \Var_{\mu_m}f=1.
\]
For \(\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}\),
\[
 D_iM_3=\frac{1-x_jx_k}{2},
\]
which vanishes on that support. All other derivatives also vanish.
Thus every flip influence is zero.

Fix any proposed six positive constants and use \(d=3,v=1\). Put
\[
 L=\left\lceil B_G3^{K_G}\right\rceil.
\]
For \(3m>L\), a uniformly random \(L\)-set satisfies
\[
 \E_S G_{\mu_m}(f,S)
 =\Pr[S\cap\{1,2,3\}\ne\varnothing]
 \le \frac{L}{m}.
\]
Indeed, hitting the first block reveals \(f\) exactly, while missing it
reveals nothing about \(f\). Choosing
\[
 m>\max\{L,\;L\,3^{C_G}/c_G\}
\]
defeats the Global alternative as well.

In contrast,
\[
 G_{\mu_m}(f,\{1\})=1.
\]
Consequently this witness satisfies the fixed-set Global alternative,
for example with
\(c_G=C_G=B_G=K_G=1\), for every admissible degree bound and variance
parameter. This is a witness-level statement; it does not assert
\(\Gamma^{\mathrm{set}}\) for the whole class.

For hereditary full AA, note that a positive-probability pinning of
\(\mu_m\) is again a product of independent laws on disjoint blocks of
at most three coordinates, some deterministic. Regard each block as one
symbol of a finite alphabet. A physical query is implemented by two
coherent symbol queries (compute the symbol, act on the requested bit,
uncompute), and a classical symbol query costs at most three physical
queries. Hence \path{foundations/product_alphabets.tex},
Corollary~\ref{cor:resolution-product-alphabet-aa}, gives every
\(T\)-query acceptance function a deterministic simulator with mean
error at most \(e\) using \(O(T^6e^{-15})\) physical queries, with an
absolute constant, uniformly over \(m\) and the pinning.
(Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold-block-products} gives the same conclusion
through a uniform-input simulator.)
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[L4: Global implies Local on products]
\label{ian:product-global-to-local}
Let \(\mu=\bigotimes_i\mu_i\), where
\(p_i=\mu_i(+1)\in[0,1]\). If \(G_\mu(f,S)\ge\gamma>0\), then
\[
 \max_i I_i^\mu(f)\ge\frac{\gamma}{|S|}.
\]
More precisely, the same lower bound holds for the maximum
heat-bath influence
\[
 H_i^\mu(f):=\E_\mu\Var(f\mid X_{-i})
            =4p_i(1-p_i)I_i^\mu(f).
\]
Hence \(\Gamma^{\mathrm{set}}\) on a product class implies ordinary
flip-influence AA, without a bias floor.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use the globally defined extension
\[
 g(x_S)=\E_{Y_{-S}\sim\mu_{-S}}f(x_S,Y_{-S}).
\]
For \(i\in S\), differentiation commutes with this integration.
Product Efron--Stein and Jensen give
\[
 \begin{split}
 \gamma
 &\le\Var_{\mu_S}g
 \le\sum_{i\in S}4p_i(1-p_i)\E_{\mu_S}(D_ig)^2\\
 &\le\sum_{i\in S}H_i^\mu(f)
 \le\sum_{i\in S}I_i^\mu(f).
 \end{split}
\]
This proves both claims.

With \(x=d/v\ge1\), the Global alternative of
Definition~\ref{ian:gamma-definition} has
\(|S|\le\lceil B_Gx^{K_G}\rceil\le(B_G+1)x^{K_G}\), so
\[
 \max_i I_i^\mu(f)
 \ge\frac{c_G}{B_G+1}(v/d)^{C_G+K_G}.
\]
Thus ordinary AA holds with
\(c=\min\{c_L,c_G/(B_G+1)\}\) and \(C=\max\{C_L,C_G+K_G\}\).
No bias floor entered, even at deterministic coordinates. A floor
\(p_i\in[a,1-a]\) is needed only for the reverse comparison
\(H_i^\mu(f)\ge4a(1-a)I_i^\mu(f)\), which would turn the flip-influence
Local branch into a heat-bath bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Hereditary full AA does not imply fixed-set Gamma]
\label{ian:gamma-set-not-necessary}
Let
\[
 \mathcal C=\operatorname{Pin}\{\mu_m:m\ge1\}
\]
be the pinning closure from
Proposition~\ref{ian:triple-random-set-failure}.
Then \(\mathcal C\) has hereditary full AA but fails
\(\Gamma^{\mathrm{set}}\).

The failure persists if the polynomial witnesses in Gamma are
restricted to quantum acceptance functions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Hereditary full AA, with the uniform \(O(T^6e^{-15})\) physical-query
bound, was proved in Proposition~\ref{ian:triple-random-set-failure}
from Corollary~\ref{cor:resolution-product-alphabet-aa}.

Proposition~\ref{ian:hard-block-addressing} below constructs, on
\(\mu_{r+2^r}\), Boolean polynomials \(f_r\) satisfying
\[
 \deg f_r\le3(r+1),\qquad
 \Var f_r=1,\qquad
 I_i(f_r)=0\quad\text{for every }i,
\]
and
\[
 G(f_r,S)\le |S|\,2^{-r}
 \quad\text{for every fixed }S.
\]
These bounds defeat every proposed set of Gamma constants.

Furthermore,
\[
 Q_r=\frac{1+f_r}{2}
\]
is an acceptance function of a deterministic algorithm: read the
three spins in each of the \(r\) address blocks, then the three spins
in the selected data block. It uses \(3(r+1)\) queries.
For \(Q_r\), the variance is \(1/4\), all influences remain zero,
and the reveal bound is divided by four, so the same asymptotic
contradiction (now with \(v=1/4\)) applies to acceptance functions
themselves. A short adaptive tree need not supply a polynomial-size
fixed revealing set.
\end{proof}
