\subsection{Thermal phase labels and Gaussian product decompositions}
\label{ian:phase-label-gibbs}

Status: proved where stated. The Hubbard--Stratonovich identity and bias formulas, the typical min-entropy bound \(N^{1-o(1)}\ge\sqrt N\) with exceptional label mass \(N^{-2}\), and the Curie--Weiss scalar representation are unconditional. The thermal restatement holds for every decomposition satisfying \eqref{ian:phase-label-component-bound}; its use with product or exchangeable components, the admissibility conclusion of Proposition~\ref{ian:phase-label-hs-admissibility}, and the Curie--Weiss positive conclusion use the uniform-input simulator \(s_U\) of Subsection~\ref{ian:phase-label-admissibility}, which Corollary~\ref{cor:resolution-product-alphabet-aa} supplies, and so rest only on inequality (BC) of Huffstutler--Miloschewsky--Podder, the sole import of that corollary; no influence form of uniform AA is used. No equivalence in terms of thermodynamic phases is formulated here, and the local structural question is recorded as an open problem (Remark~\ref{ian:phase-label-local-open-problem}).

Fix a temperature-independent classical Hamiltonian family \(E_N\) and
\(\beta>0\), and let \(\mu_{\beta,N}\propto e^{-\beta E_N}\). Constants may
depend on \(\beta\) and fixed interaction parameters, not on
\(N,T,\varepsilon\). For \(H\subseteq\Omega_N\) put
\(F_H(\beta)=-\beta^{-1}\log\sum_{x\in H}e^{-\beta E_N(x)}\) and
\(F(\beta)=F_{\Omega_N}(\beta)\), the sums running over all physical
spins.

\begin{theorem}[Thermal restatement]
\label{ian:phase-label-thermal-theorem}
Fix a decomposition of \(\mu_{\beta,N}\) satisfying
\eqref{ian:phase-label-component-bound} with polynomial \(\Psi_\beta\).
The following are equivalent:
(1) full AA fails at \(\beta\);
(2) the programs \(\widehat Q\) violate
\eqref{ian:phase-label-program-eliminability} for every polynomial \(\Phi\);
(3) for all \(\theta_1,\theta_2\ge1\) there are witnesses
\eqref{ian:phase-label-hard-witness}, which then satisfy
\eqref{ian:phase-label-lifted-signal}--\eqref{ian:phase-label-explained-signal-hidden}.
Every such witness has
\[
 F_H(\beta)-F(\beta)
 =\frac1\beta\log\frac1{\mu_{\beta,N}(H)}
 \le\frac1\beta\log\frac{64}{\varepsilon^3}.
 \tag{21.1}\label{ian:phase-label-thermal-gap}
\]
If moreover
\[
 \E\Var(Q\mid Z)\le\frac{\mu_{\beta,N}(H)}{4096}\Bigl(\frac{\varepsilon^2}{4}\Bigr)^2,
 \tag{21.2}\label{ian:phase-label-thermal-flatness}
\]
the label-only versions of Proposition~\ref{ian:phase-label-flat-components} hold.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Theorems~\ref{ian:phase-label-elimination} and
\ref{ian:phase-label-hard-sector-converse}, and
Proposition~\ref{ian:phase-label-flat-components}; the gap identity is
\(\mu_{\beta,N}(H)=\sum_{x\in H}e^{-\beta E_N(x)}/\sum_xe^{-\beta E_N(x)}\).
\end{proof}

The anti-atom condition \eqref{ian:phase-label-anti-atom} is never used.
The theorem therefore holds for \(Z=X\), whose point-mass components are
simulated with zero queries, so that \(\widehat Q=Q\) and (2) merely
restates (1): it is
the content of Section~\ref{ian:phase-label-theorem} for Gibbs laws, and
anything beyond that lies in the choice of decomposition. An equivalence
whose labels are thermodynamic phases is not formulated here. In
particular infinite-volume extremality (pure DLR states) or clustering
does not by itself give the uniform finite-volume bound
\eqref{ian:phase-label-component-bound}; a finite-volume phase partition
would also have to specify boundary conditions, exceptional
configurations, and its simulation bound.

\begin{proposition}[Hubbard--Stratonovich product decomposition]
\label{ian:phase-label-hubbard-stratonovich}
Let
\(\mu(x)\propto\exp\bigl(\frac\beta2x^\mathsf TJx+\beta\varphi^\mathsf Tx\bigr)\)
with \(J\) real symmetric and field \(\varphi\in\mathbb R^N\). Choose a
diagonal \(D\) with \(K=J+D\succeq0\); this changes the energy by a
constant on \(\Omega_N\). Let \(\gamma_{\beta K}\) be the (possibly
singular) centered Gaussian law with covariance \(\beta K\), and
\[
 \pi_{\rm HS}(dg)
 =\frac{\prod_{i=1}^N2\cosh(\beta\varphi_i+g_i)}
         {\mathcal N_K}\,\gamma_{\beta K}(dg),
 \qquad
 \mu_g(x)
 =\prod_{i=1}^N
       \frac{e^{(\beta\varphi_i+g_i)x_i}}
            {2\cosh(\beta\varphi_i+g_i)},
 \tag{21.3}\label{ian:phase-label-hs-mixture}
\]
with \(\mathcal N_K\) the normalizer. Then \(\mu=\int\mu_g\,\pi_{\rm HS}(dg)\),
the component probabilities are
\[
 p_i(g)=\frac1{1+e^{-2(\beta\varphi_i+g_i)}},
 \tag{21.4}\label{ian:phase-label-hs-bias}
\]
and they all lie in \([\alpha,1-\alpha]\) exactly when
\[
 \max_i|\beta\varphi_i+g_i|
 \le \frac12\log\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}.
 \tag{21.5}\label{ian:phase-label-hs-bias-event}
\]
If \(K_{ii}>0\), no fixed \(\alpha>0\) bounds coordinate \(i\) over the
whole label support.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\(\int e^{g^\mathsf Tx}\,\gamma_{\beta K}(dg)=e^{\beta x^\mathsf TKx/2}\);
multiply by \(e^{\beta\varphi^\mathsf Tx}\), sum over \(x\), and normalize.
The bias assertions are the logistic formula; the cosh tilt has positive
density relative to \(\gamma_{\beta K}\), so the unbounded Gaussian tails
of coordinates with \(K_{ii}>0\) remain.
\end{proof}

The mixing law \(\pi_{\rm HS}\) is a tilted Gaussian, not a Gaussian.
Positive semidefiniteness is no restriction: every finite symmetric
\(J\) admits such a shift. For comparison with the standard transform
see Theorem 3.12 of the following source; the identities needed here
were proved above.
% Literature: [Liu--Mohanty--Rajaraman--Wu, Fast Mixing in Sparse Random Ising Models](https://amitrajaraman.github.io/publications/sparse-ising.pdf).

\begin{proposition}[Typical entropy of Gaussian product components]
\label{ian:phase-label-hs-admissibility}
Suppose, uniformly in \(N\),
\(\max_i|\varphi_i|\le\varphi_*\),
\(\max_i\sum_j|K_{ij}|\le\Lambda\) and
\(\max_i\beta K_{ii}\le\sigma_*^2\). Put
\(B_N=\beta(\varphi_*+\Lambda)+\sqrt{2\sigma_*^2\log(2N^3)}\) and
\(\alpha_N=(1+e^{2B_N})^{-1}=e^{-O(\sqrt{\log N})}\). Then
\[
 \pi_{\rm HS}\Bigl\{g:\max_i|\beta\varphi_i+g_i|>B_N\Bigr\}\le N^{-2},
\]
and off this set all component probabilities lie in
\([\alpha_N,1-\alpha_N]\) and
\(H_\infty(\mu_g)\ge N\alpha_N=N^{1-o(1)}\ge\sqrt N\) for large \(N\).
Hence \eqref{ian:phase-label-hs-mixture} satisfies
\eqref{ian:phase-label-anti-atom} unconditionally and, using
\(s_U\), is \(\mathcal C_\cup\)-admissible with the product
polynomial \eqref{ian:phase-label-product-polynomial}. Every
bounded-degree, bounded-strength pairwise Ising family with bounded
fields has such a decomposition.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the joint Gaussian--spin representation, completing the square gives
\(G\mid X=x\sim\mathcal N(\beta Kx,\beta K)\), so
\(|\beta\varphi_i+\E[G_i\mid X]|\le\beta(\varphi_*+\Lambda)\). A Gaussian
tail bound and a union bound over \(i\), conditional on \(X\) and then
averaged, give exceptional mass at most
\(2N\exp(-\log(2N^3))=N^{-2}\) (if \(\sigma_*=0\) there is no
exceptional set). Off it,
\(-\log\max_x\mu_g(x)\ge-N\log(1-\alpha_N)\ge N\alpha_N\), and
\(\alpha_N\ge\frac12e^{-2B_N}\) with \(B_N=O(\sqrt{\log N})\). All
components, exceptional ones included, are products, so
Proposition~\ref{ian:phase-label-union-class} applies. For bounded row
sums of \(J\), a fixed scalar shift makes \(K\succeq0\) by Gershgorin and
keeps the row sums and diagonal bounded.
\end{proof}

The bias bound \(\alpha_N\) depends on dimension; it is the
arbitrary-bias product theorem that keeps the simulation polynomial
dimension-free. The proposition applies to the repo's bounded-degree
pairwise counterexamples, Theorems~\ref{res:pairwise-theorem} and
\ref{res:zero-field-theorem}, which fail full AA in their stated
low-temperature regimes. So a product-mixture
decomposition with component min-entropy at least \(\sqrt N\) (indeed
\(N^{1-o(1)}\)) outside label mass \(N^{-2}\), together with bounded
local interactions, is compatible with full-AA failure.

Further Gibbs families admit certified simulation bounds
\eqref{ian:phase-label-component-bound} with the whole law as one
component; the anti-atom condition is not checked here. Products of
bounded-size blocks (Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold-block-products}),
exchangeable families (\ref{exch:heading-2}) and Ising forests
(Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold-ising-forests}) do so through the
uniform-input simulator \(s_U\), which
Corollary~\ref{cor:resolution-product-alphabet-aa} supplies. The bounded
local families in the high-temperature regime of
Theorem~\ref{thm:resolution-dynamics-arbitrary-degree} do so through
the same corollary (with its imported external lemma) applied
directly, not through \(s_U\).

For Curie--Weiss the scalar Gaussian representation has mixing density
\(\propto\exp(-nt^2/(2\beta))(2\cosh t)^n\,dt\) and conditional product
probability \((1+\tanh t)/2\); at \(\beta>1\) its modes are
\(t=\pm\beta m_\beta\). The field support is unbounded, and the positive
conclusion of Proposition~\ref{ian:phase-label-positive-instances}, which
uses \(s_U\), comes from exchangeability and phase
learnability, not from a fixed bias bound on this representation.

\begin{remark}[Open problem: local character-order structure]
\label{ian:phase-label-local-open-problem}
\emph{Intended idea.} In bounded-degree, bounded-strength local Gibbs
laws, a phase-label obstruction should come from a code-like label:
thermally populated, hidden from classical trees whose depth is
independent of \(N\), and quantumly relevant, as the character index
(with the sign, \((A,B)\), in signed families) is in the repo's local
codes. It is not hidden at depth \(O(\log N)\); see the first correction.

\emph{Two corrections any formulation must respect.} First, the
character label is determined by \(m=\log_2n\) clean coordinates
(\(m+1\) with the sign), and in
the noisy neighborhood (fewer than \(n/32\) errors) it is learnable from
\(O(\log n+\log(1/\delta))\) uniformly sampled coordinates with
unrestricted decoding: the true signed character has mismatch rate below
\(1/32\), every other above \(15/32\), and Hoeffding with a union bound
over \(2n\) candidates suffices. So ``not determined by any
\(O(\log N)\) spins'' excludes the intended examples; the relevant scale
is polynomial in \(T\) and \(\varepsilon^{-1}\), independent of \(N\), and
a \(\Theta(\log N)\) lower bound at fixed \(T,\varepsilon\) already
violates full AA. Second, every parity of the label is one classical
query in the clean oracle; the separated observable is the nonlinear
\(h(A)\) (remark after
Proposition~\ref{ian:phase-label-character-noisy-instances}). So ``one
quantum query reveals a parity of the label'' does not express the
obstruction, and the definition of full AA does not require \(T=1\).

\emph{Surviving weaker form.} For such a family failing full AA: on a
polynomial refinement \(\nu'\le\nu/\kappa\) of a hard witness \(\nu\),
with \(\kappa\) inverse-polynomial in the witness parameters, is there a
near-uniform label \(\lambda:\operatorname{supp}\nu'\to\mathbb F_2^m\)
with \(m\ge c\log t\) whose basis characters \((-1)^{e_i\cdot\lambda}\)
are each \(\kappa\)-correlated with some \(k_0\)-local observable, with
\(c,k_0\) fixed? The hard-witness part alone already follows from
Remark~\ref{ian:hard-sector-polynomial-quantifiers} with the constants
\(64,3,2\) there (its \(K,p,q\); unrelated to the coupling matrix \(K\)).
As stated the question is weak: a label made of \(m\) spin values meets
the correlation clause with \(k_0=1\), so a useful version must also
force \(\lambda\) to be hidden, item (e) below.

\emph{Requirements for a stronger formulation.} (a) Fix the scope: a
fixed Hamiltonian sequence or a class, and whether fields count in the
strength bound. (b) Quantify \(t,a,w,m\) and the observable index
explicitly. (c) Any redundancy condition on the correlated observables
must not be met by rescaled copies of a single observable. (d) A
relevance condition such as \(\Var_{\nu'}\E[Q\mid\lambda]\ge\kappa\) must
not be obtainable merely by choosing the refinement \(\nu'\). (e)
\(\lambda\) must be hidden from shallow trees, which
\(m\ge c\log t\) alone does not force, since it allows \(m\ll t\). Possible counterexamples include
Forrelation-type mechanisms based on Fourier correlation rather than
recovery of a character index; no bounded-degree thermal implementation
with every spin queryable is known here.
% Literature: [Aaronson--Ambainis, Forrelation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5729).
\end{remark}
