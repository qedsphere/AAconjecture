\subsection{Theorem}\label{exch:heading-2}

Assume uniform-input AA: every \(T\)-query quantum acceptance function
has a deterministic classical approximation under the uniform input law
with average absolute error \(\epsilon\), using at most
\(S_0(T,1/\epsilon)\) queries for a fixed polynomial \(S_0\).

Then there is a polynomial \(R(T,1/\epsilon)\) such that, for every
\(n\) and every \(T\)-query quantum acceptance function
\(Q:\{0,1\}^n\to[0,1]\), there is \textbf{one} deterministic classical
algorithm \(D\) with at most \(R(T,1/\epsilon)\) queries satisfying

\[
\mathbb E_{X\sim U_{n,k}}|D(X)-Q(X)|\le\epsilon
\quad\text{simultaneously for every }k=0,\ldots,n.
\]

Here \(U_{n,k}\) is uniform on the Hamming slice of weight \(k\).
Consequently the same \(D\) works for every permutation-invariant input
law, since every such law is a mixture of these slices.

In particular, Curie--Weiss input laws admit polynomial classical
acceptance simulation at every inverse temperature, including
dimension-dependent temperatures and the zero-temperature symmetric
phase mixture. This remains true where their local-influence AA versions
fail.

This proof is a deduction recorded for the investigation; no novelty
claim is made. Quantitative biased-product simulation and slice
couplings also appear in
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35327v1}{Girish--Mittal--Nehoran--Raz,
Propositions 3.6 and 3.10, Remark 3.4}. The proof below derives the
needed ingredients.
