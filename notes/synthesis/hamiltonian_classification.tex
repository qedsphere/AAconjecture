\section{Hamiltonian classification and temperature table}
\label{sec:hamiltonian-classification}

This table assembles the previously dispersed model results and adds
the tree-partition consequences of
Section~\ref{sec:classification-tree-partitions}, endpoint improvements
for the local code constructions, and arbitrary prescribed global
thresholds and failure windows. The new deductions are recorded on
14 November 2026; they have explicit arguments and finite checks but
have not received an independent audit. Positive statements retain
the imported uniform-AA or product-alphabet completion inputs of
their cited proofs. No claim of a complete classification or research
priority is made.

\paragraph{Conventions needed to read the table.}
The target is polynomial classical \emph{query} simulation of every
quantum acceptance function, with polynomial dependence on $T$ and
$1/\epsilon$ independent of dimension. No computation-time guarantee
is asserted. We classify families $H_n$, with a specified energy
normalization and, for quantum systems, a specified measurement.
Unless expressly stated otherwise, $\beta\ge0$ and all structural
parameters are fixed as $n\to\infty$. Write
\[
 \Lambda=\sup_n\max_i\sum_j|J_{ij}|,\quad
 W=\sup_n\max_i\sum_{A\ni i,\,|A|\ge2}(|A|-1)|J_A|,
 \quad c_k=\frac{e^{-k(2+\frac12\log2)}}{32ek}.
\]
The symbol $J_*$ bounds each remaining pair coupling. All classical
positive graph results allow arbitrary finite fields and coupling
signs. ``All finite'' means every fixed $0\le\beta<\infty$, not
a uniform assertion for $\beta_n\to\infty$. The endpoint $\infty$
means the canonical Gibbs ground-state limit where it is included.
``Open here'' records the scope of these notes, not a claim that no
result exists anywhere in the literature. A sufficient endpoint is
not called an exact critical temperature.

Here $b_0=12289\log2/64$, and the quantum incident norm is

Classical diagonal Hamiltonians measured in their diagonal product
basis inherit the corresponding classical rows. A noncommuting
Hamiltonian on a tree or strip does not automatically inherit the
classical all-finite-temperature result. For the independent quantum
component row, tensor factorization of the Gibbs state and a product
measurement produce independent blocks of bounded size; this proves
that row directly from Theorem~\ref{thm:threshold-block-products}.

\subsection{Including the boundary in two local failure regimes}
\label{sec:classification-local-endpoints}

For the pairwise code family of Theorem~\ref{res:pairwise-theorem}, let
\[
 K(t)=\frac12\bigl(4\log\cosh(2t)-\log\cosh(4t)\bigr).
\]
It is strictly increasing for $t>0$, starts at zero, and is unbounded.
Thus $K(t_*)=\log2/32$ has one positive solution. The existing proof
gives, without a strict-temperature assumption,
\[
 \delta_n\le\frac1n\exp\bigl((\log2-32K(\beta j))n\bigr).
\]
At equality $K(\beta j)=\log2/32$, this is $\delta_n\le1/n\to0$.
The same query lower bound
\[
 \mathbb E|D-P|\ge(1-\delta_n)
       \left[\frac{1-2^{3t-m/2}}2-\frac18\right]
\]
therefore still forces $t=\Omega(\log n)$ at constant error.
Exponential convergence was stronger than necessary. Consequently
the sufficient failure condition may be strengthened from $>$ to
$\ge$, with $t_*\simeq0.2447170446$. This is not a proof that $t_*$
is the exact simulation threshold.

Likewise, Theorem~\ref{res:zero-field-theorem} supplies
\[
 \delta_n\le\frac1{2n}
  \exp\bigl(((1+6C)\log2-64\beta j)n\bigr),\qquad C=2048.
\]
At $\beta j=(1+6C)\log2/64$, the upper bound is $1/(2n)$,
and the same lower-bound argument applies. The zero-field sufficient
failure condition also includes its endpoint. Both constructions
retain bounded degree and incident strength, and every auxiliary
spin remains queryable. Neither is an asserted lower bound for
all Ising models with those degree and strength bounds.

\subsection{Prescribing a global threshold or a failure window}
\label{sec:classification-prescribed-window}

The value of an exact threshold cannot be discussed without energy
normalization. If the character-code penalty Hamiltonian from
Section~\ref{qm:model-and-theorem} is divided by $\beta_c>0$,
its effective inverse temperature is $\beta/\beta_c$. Its exact
simulation region becomes $[0,\beta_c)$ and its failure region
$[\beta_c,\infty]$. The ground-state limit is the uniform code law,
so the same one-query separation persists at $\infty$.

More generally, choose any fixed $0<a<b<\infty$, let $\mathcal C_n$
be the size-$n$ character code, and distinguish its all-plus word
$c_0$. Define
\[
 H_n(x)=\begin{cases}
 0,&x=c_0,\\
 (\log n)/b,&x\in\mathcal C_n\setminus\{c_0\},\\
 (n\log2)/a,&x\notin\mathcal C_n.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{CW1}
\]
This has the exact simulation set
\[
 \boxed{[0,a)\ \cup\ (b,\infty]},\qquad
 \text{and exact failure set }[a,b].
 \tag{CW2}
\]
To verify this, use the exact partition function
\[
 Z_n=1+(n-1)n^{-\beta/b}+(2^n-n)2^{-\beta n/a}.
\]
For fixed $\beta<a$, the bulk dominates exponentially, and the
Gibbs law is within $C_\beta/n$ of uniform after enlarging the
constant to cover finitely many small $n$. Uniform simulation at
error $\epsilon/2$ suffices when this distance is at most
$\epsilon/2$; otherwise querying all $n\le2C_\beta/\epsilon$
spins suffices. For $a\le\beta\le b$, the law dominates uniform
code measure with weight
$n^{1-\beta/b}/Z_n$, tending to one for $a\le\beta<b$ and
to $1/2$ at $\beta=b$. The existing code query lower bound applies.
For $\beta>b$, the mass outside $c_0$ is at most
$C_\beta n^{-(\beta/b-1)}$. Outputting $Q(c_0)$ or reading the
whole input, according to whether this bound is at most $\epsilon$,
uses polynomially many queries. At $\infty$ the input is $c_0$
deterministically. This repeats the original nonmonotone proof with
two independent energy scales, and supplies the exact endpoints.
It is a global-interaction construction, not a bounded-locality example.

\subsection{A practical order of classification}

For a proposed family, first fix normalization, the input measurement,
and whether temperature is fixed or dimension dependent. Then check
independent bounded components and fixed-species symmetry; these can
settle every temperature immediately. Next seek a tree-partition
into bounded-size bags, possibly after reading a bounded exceptional
set. For remaining classical families compute $\Lambda$ or $W$,
and for quantum families compute locality $k$ and $\Lambda_q$.
These quantities supply positive regions, not negative conclusions
outside those regions. To claim failure, exhibit an actual quantum
acceptance function and a classical query lower bound with all
physical coordinates accessible. To claim an exact threshold, close
the remaining interval and check its endpoints.

Bounded-degree graphs with bounded treewidth are now classified at
all finite temperatures by the new argument. Natural next targets
include bounded-treewidth graphs with unbounded degree outside the
already covered cases, general ferromagnetic graphs, and concrete
two-dimensional lattices. The full Dobrushin extension remains a
sufficient-region conjecture, not a proposed universal sharp threshold.
