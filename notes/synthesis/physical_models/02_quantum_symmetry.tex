\subsection{Noncommuting mean-field Hamiltonians have no full-AA temperature boundary}
\label{threshold_physics:quantum_symmetry}

For an \(n\)-qubit Hamiltonian \(\mathcal H_n\), the input law used here
is the outcome of measuring its Gibbs state in the computational basis:
\[
 \mu_{n,\beta}(x)=
 \frac{\langle x|e^{-\beta\mathcal H_n}|x\rangle}
 {\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\beta\mathcal H_n})}.
\]
It is generally different from exponentiating the diagonal entries of
\(\mathcal H_n\), as explained in Section~\ref{proof:quantum_interactions}.

\begin{corollary}[Symmetry of the Hamiltonian suffices at every temperature]
\label{cor:quantum_species}
Suppose \(P_\pi\mathcal H_nP_\pi^*=\mathcal H_n\) for every permutation
\(\pi\) preserving each block of a known \(r\)-species partition. Then
the measured Gibbs input law has full AA with the bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:species_simulation}. For fixed \(r\), this is uniform in
\(\beta\), in the Hamiltonian's coefficients, and in dimension-dependent
temperature schedules. It includes the canonical zero-temperature limit.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Functional calculus gives
\(P_\pi e^{-\beta\mathcal H_n}P_\pi^*=e^{-\beta\mathcal H_n}\).
Since \(P_\pi|x\rangle=|\pi x\rangle\), the measured law is invariant
under every such permutation. Apply the species theorem. At zero
temperature the Gibbs state converges to the normalized projector onto
the ground space; that projector has the same symmetry. The simultaneous
slice simulator is unchanged, so its guarantee passes to this limit.
\end{proof}

This does not cover an arbitrarily selected symmetry-breaking pure
ground state: that is a different input preparation. Measuring every
site in the same rotated single-qubit basis, or using one common basis
per species, also preserves the relevant permutation symmetry.

In particular the collective transverse-field Ising family
\[
 \mathcal H_n=-\frac{J}{2n}\left(\sum_iZ_i\right)^2
              -\Gamma\sum_iX_i-h\sum_iZ_i
\]
has full AA for all \(J,\Gamma,h,\beta\), including parameters depending
on \(n\). The terms need not commute. The conclusion extends to arbitrary
permutation-invariant polynomials in collective spin operators, and to
fixed-species collective couplings. For example, with
\(S_b^\alpha=\sum_{i\in B_b}\sigma_i^\alpha\), arbitrary self-adjoint
expressions in the \(S_b^\alpha\) have the required symmetry.

The model nevertheless has a genuine thermodynamic phase boundary.
For \(J>0\), \(h=0\), its zero-magnetization boundary is
\[
 \frac{J}{\Gamma}\tanh(\beta\Gamma)=1,
 \qquad\text{with the limit }\beta J=1\text{ at }\Gamma=0.
\]
This follows by rescaling the normalization in
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.1605}{Chayes--Crawford--Ioffe--Levit,
Theorem 3.2 and equation (3.7)}. The single-spin susceptibility
\(\chi_0=\tanh(\beta\Gamma)/\Gamma\) is also obtained directly by
differentiating
\[
 \langle Z\rangle_{-\Gamma X-uZ}
 =\frac{u}{\sqrt{\Gamma^2+u^2}}
   \tanh\!\left(\beta\sqrt{\Gamma^2+u^2}\right)
\]
at \(u=0\). For the full-AA question, both sides of this physical
boundary are in the same proven positive region. A phase transition is
therefore not sufficient to predict an acceptance-simulation threshold.
