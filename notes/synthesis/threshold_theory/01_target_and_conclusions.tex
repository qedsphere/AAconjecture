\subsection{The target and the proposed organizing principle}
\label{tht:target}

In this investigation, \emph{full AA} means the acceptance-simulation
property: for every $T$-query quantum acceptance function
$Q:\{-1,1\}^n\to[0,1]$ and every $\epsilon>0$, a classical query
algorithm $D$ satisfies
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mu_n}|D(X)-Q(X)|\le\epsilon,
 \qquad \operatorname{queries}(D)\le P(T,1/\epsilon),
\]
where $P$ is a polynomial independent of $n$. We may take $D$ to be a
deterministic tree with real outputs in $[0,1]$: auxiliary randomness
can be fixed by averaging. This terminology follows the task's primary
target; in the literature, AA often specifically names the stronger
bounded-polynomial influence statement. The distinction is essential.
Replacing $\epsilon$ by $\epsilon\delta$ gives additive error $\epsilon$
outside a set of measure at most $\delta$. Computation and preprocessing
are unrestricted; sampling a fresh input does not approximate a function
of the particular unknown input supplied to the algorithm.

Positive results import uniform-input AA, as proved in
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09773}{Turturean's November 2026 preprint}
and developed in the supplied exposition. The product/slice transfer
background is
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35327}{Girish--Mittal--Nehoran--Raz}.
New deductions below are proved within these notes; research priority
and formal verification are not claimed.

The proposed organizing principle is this:
\begin{quote}
A full-AA threshold can arise when cooling gives appreciable weight to
collective information that a few coherent oracle queries can read but
polynomially many classical coordinate queries cannot infer. Ordinary
ordering is insufficient. On the positive side, a local representation
must remain accessible after conditioning on the observed input, or
symmetry must make the relevant collective information classically
estimable.
\end{quote}
This is an interpretation supported by the theorems below, not a proved
classification of all Hamiltonians. A physical model must specify a
sequence $H_n$, interaction normalization, measurement basis if quantum,
and fixed versus dimension-dependent temperature parameters.

Three results give the interpretation concrete content. First, the
existing exchangeable-input theorem and its extension to finitely many
species imply full AA at \emph{every} temperature for fully connected
permutation-symmetric classical and transverse-field quantum models.
Second, the posterior-access argument below proves full AA for Ising
forests at every fixed finite temperature with bounded edge strengths,
including arbitrary external fields. Thus even branching, long-range
order, or a thermodynamic transition need not obstruct simulation.
Third, the noisy-character construction below gives a single extensive
Hamiltonian family with bounded one-spin energy changes for which full
AA fails at \emph{every fixed} $\beta>0$. Its global interaction order is
unbounded. This separates weak local energy changes from genuinely local
interaction structure.

The genuine finite positive threshold already established in the dossier
is the character-code penalty family: full AA holds for fixed $\beta<1$
and fails for $\beta\ge1$. Its energy--entropy balance makes a hard code
sector thermally visible. The numerical value one depends on the stated
energy normalization. These first examples have global interactions.
The later resolution sections prove a high-temperature weighted-interaction
criterion, extend it to measured noncommuting local Hamiltonians, and
exhibit bounded-degree pairwise Ising families with full-AA failure at
low temperature. Section~\ref{sec:resolution-nonmonotone} also proves
that the positive temperature set need not be an interval.
