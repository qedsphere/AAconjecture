\subsection{What is established, and what remains a research question}
\label{sec:resolution-status}

The target throughout is acceptance simulation by a deterministic
classical tree of depth polynomial in quantum query count and inverse
mean error, uniformly in input dimension. Positive assertions import
uniform-input AA as stated in Section~\ref{tht:target}. They do not
assert efficient classical computation. For a fixed finite Hamiltonian
the query question is vacuous after reading all spins; the substantive
statements concern families as dimension grows.

The proved structural picture is as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Independent bounded-size components and a fixed number of
permutation-symmetric spin species satisfy full AA at every
temperature, by the product and simultaneous-slice simulation
theorems. The latter includes the previously stated symmetric quantum
families measured in the specified common product basis. Classical
Ising forests satisfy it at every fixed finite temperature with
uniformly bounded edge strength, even with arbitrary degree and fields.
The subsequent tree-partition argument in
Section~\ref{sec:classification-tree-partitions} extends this to
cactus graphs, fixed-width strips, fixed-range one-dimensional
models, and bounded-treewidth graphs with bounded maximum degree,
at every fixed finite temperature with bounded edge strengths.
These new deductions are separate from the earlier audit.
\item Classical interactions have a uniform positive region
$\beta\max_i\sum_{A\ni i,\,|A|\ge2}(|A|-1)|J_A|\le1/8$,
by Theorem~\ref{thm:resolution-dynamics-arbitrary-degree}, without
a degree or arity restriction and with arbitrary fields. Its source is
subcritical propagation of query dependencies, including after
conditioning on the entire observed input.
For pairwise interactions, the subsequent derivation in
Section~\ref{resolution-dynamics:dobrushin-attempt} improves this
sufficient constant to $1/5$, still with arbitrary degree and fields.
The full Dobrushin regime remains open; the new derivation is not
part of the earlier recorded audit.
\item For general, possibly noncommuting $k$-local quantum Hamiltonians,
binary product measurements satisfy full AA whenever
$\beta\Lambda\le e^{-k(2+\frac12\log2)}/(32ek)$, where
$\Lambda=\max_i\sum_{A\ni i}\|h_A\|$ includes physical one-site
terms (Corollary~\ref{cor:quantum-clusters-full-aa}). The proof controls
every effective classical interaction via a convergent expansion.
\item Bounded-degree pairwise Ising Hamiltonians can violate full AA
at low temperature, even with every auxiliary spin queryable
(Theorem~\ref{res:pairwise-theorem}). Thus bounded interaction order,
degree, or incident strength alone is not an all-temperature criterion.
\item Without locality, a fixed soft-code Hamiltonian fails at every
fixed $\beta>0$ (Section~\ref{threshold:obstructions}), while
the preceding three-level example has a disconnected positive
temperature set. At $\beta=0$ every finite Hamiltonian gives uniform
measurement outcomes and therefore satisfies the imported uniform AA
theorem. Consequently no finite Hamiltonian family can fail at every
$\beta\ge0$ in this convention.
\end{enumerate}

The obstruction is not order or a thermodynamic singularity by itself:
the positive symmetric examples may order. The explicit negative
families retain a growing hidden code label that a coherent query can
test while a few classical coordinate answers cannot identify enough
of it. The positive constructions instead expose a source whose
queries can be simulated locally, including its posterior given the
physical input.

This also answers the generalized-influence question constructively.
The high-temperature proof uses influences of random update maps and
their selected parent sets, and bounds their expected size even under
posterior conditioning. It is therefore a successful source-based
replacement for physical single-spin influence. For more general
laws, the reveal-progress criterion of
Section~\ref{tht:reveal} gives the exact hereditary simulation
criterion; bounded-size physical flip or resampling influence is too
restrictive, as shown by the ordered mean-field and parity examples.

These results do not give exact critical temperatures for the sparse
code Hamiltonians, an all-temperature classification of ferromagnetic
graphs, or the maximal high-temperature regime of measured quantum
Gibbs states. Those questions remain distinct from the proved positive
regions and counterexamples. The notes therefore do not claim a
complete classification of arbitrary Hamiltonians.

The normalization matters for the phrase ``no positive temperature
works.'' Rescaling the bounded-degree pairwise counterexample by
$\log N$ makes it fail at every fixed $\beta>0$, but lets its coupling
strengths grow with dimension. In contrast, the theorems above rule
out such all-positive-$\beta$ failure for fixed-order families with
uniformly bounded incident strength, including noncommuting ones
under product measurement. The previously constructed global soft-code
example instead has bounded spin-flip energy changes and unbounded
interaction order.
