\subsection{A reusable criterion for shrinking latent
mixtures}\label{proof:positive_structure:a-reusable-criterion-for-shrinking-latent-mixtures}

Suppose \(\mu _n\) is a mixture of product-spin laws with common random
mean \(T_n\), and assume, with fixed constants:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \(T_n\) is symmetric and \(\mathbb{E} T_n^2\le C n^{-\alpha }\) for
  some \(\alpha >0\).
\item
  Its density is at least \(\eta /a_n\) on \([-a_n,a_n]\), with
  \(a_n \in (0,1]\) and fixed \(\eta >0\).
\item
  \(\operatorname{Var}_\mu  g\le C n^\gamma  \sum_i I_i^\mu (g)\) for
  every real \(g\), with \(\gamma \ge 0\).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Theorem P4 (latent-mixture transfer).} These conditions imply

\[
\max_i I_i^{\mu_n}(f)\ge c\frac{v^A}{d^B},
\quad A=\max\left\{\frac52,1+\frac{\gamma+1}{\alpha}\right\},
\quad B=\max\left\{8,\frac{4(\gamma+1)}{\alpha}\right\}.
\tag{P4}
\]

\textbf{Proof.} For bounded degree-\(r\) \(h\), the function
\(q(t)=\mathbb{E}_{P_t}h\) is a bounded univariate degree-\(r\)
polynomial, so \(\lVert q''\rVert\le r^4\). Symmetry gives
\(|\mathbb{E}_\mu  h-\mathbb{E}_U h|\le C r^4n^{-\alpha }\) and hence
\(|\operatorname{Var}_\mu  f-\operatorname{Var}_U f|\le C d^4n^{-\alpha }\).

For the nonnegative polynomial \(q_i(t)=\mathbb{E}_{P_t}(D_i f)^2\),
central density and the rescaled Markov inequality give
\(I_i^\mu \ge c I_i^U/d^2\): its integral over the central interval is
at least a constant times \(a_n q_i(0)/d^2\). The complete polynomial
integral lemma is in Section~\ref{proof:high_temperature}.

When \(n\ge (C d^4/v)^{1/\alpha }\), variance comparison and UAA give
\(I_{\max}\ge c v^{5/2}/d^8\). Below that cutoff, Poincaré gives

\(I_{\max}\ge v/(C n^{\gamma +1})\ge c v^{1+(\gamma +1)/\alpha }/d^{4(\gamma +1)/\alpha }\).

Both imply P4 because \(v\le 1\) and \(d\ge 1\). \qed

Normalized zero-field Curie--Weiss at \(0<\beta \le 1\) has
\(\alpha =\gamma =1/2\), recovering the sharp variance exponent four and
degree exponent twelve. The endpoint \(\beta =0\) is uniform and is
handled directly; its degenerate latent variable has no density of the
type assumed above. This criterion isolates latent concentration,
central mass, and a controlled variance inequality rather than treating
temperature alone as decisive.
