\subsection{A testable high-temperature programme, with precise limits}
\label{threshold_physics:conjectures}

\textbf{Classical contraction conjecture.}
Fix \(k\) and \(\kappa>0\). Every family of classical \(k\)-local Gibbs
laws with
\[
 \beta\max_i\sum_{A\ni i}(|A|-1)|J_A|\le1-\kappa
\]
has full distributional AA, with polynomial query overhead independent
of dimension; the polynomial may depend on \(k,\kappa\). Arbitrary
one-site fields are allowed. This is a proposed sufficient condition,
not an asserted sharp boundary and not an implication proved by the
Dobrushin coefficient calculation alone.

\textbf{Quantum high-temperature conjecture.}
There is a universal small constant \(c>0\) such that, for every fixed
locality \(k\), every measured Pauli Gibbs family with per-site
strength \(s\) and \(\beta ks<c\) has full AA in a fixed product
measurement basis. Constants may depend on the slack below the stated
bound. This remains a research conjecture here. Separability or
approximate Markovianity supplies motivation, not the missing proof.

A useful proof would expose the particular oracle's latent thermal
randomness locally. Specifically, it should provide a source of
independent variables, a way to evaluate requested spins from that
source, and a way to sample the source \emph{conditional on the actual
queried input} without examining a growing number of other spins.
The necessary conditional-access property is stronger than a forward
Gibbs sampler. All estimates must survive the adaptive transcripts of
the proposed simulator.

Approximate local constructions must also control the correct error.
A guarantee \(\Pr[X_i\ne\widetilde X_i]\le\eta\) only after averaging
over \(X\) is insufficient for the standard hybrid argument: a quantum
algorithm may place its query mass on input-dependent exceptional
coordinates. A safe target is
\[
 \sup_x\max_i\Pr[X_i\ne\widetilde X_i\mid X=x]\le\eta,
\]
or a directly proved bound on the query-weighted error. This gives a
\(4T\sqrt\eta\) acceptance error without a union bound over all \(n\)
spins. Requiring total variation approximation of the whole input
instead typically introduces a dimension-dependent cost.

The best discriminating experiments and proofs would test the following
contrasts.
\begin{enumerate}
\item
Hold the local strength and locality fixed while destroying species
symmetry. Full AA already holds at every temperature before this change;
criticality alone predicts no loss of it.
\item
Compare a readily estimable collective order parameter with an encoded
label whose coordinate answers reveal only linear constraints. The
former is covered by slice simulation, whereas the latter can support
the character-code separation.
\item
Track both the Gibbs weight of an encoded sector and the classical
query cost of its predicate. Energy--entropy competition matters only
if the thermally populated sector also has a genuine query separation.
\item
For proposed local encodings, expose every ancilla as an oracle
coordinate during the lower-bound analysis. An auxiliary spin that
stores the answer can erase the separation. A marginal-input convention
that hides ancillas is a different model and must be declared.
\end{enumerate}

Thus physical mixing and loss of order are useful candidates for
\emph{sufficient} positive-temperature scales. They cannot supply a
universal necessary boundary. The full-AA obstruction sought is a
thermally significant, classically inaccessible encoded label; symmetry
and local conditional access are mechanisms that prevent this
obstruction even in ordered or noncommuting models.
