\subsection{Energy--entropy competition and the mass of a hard sector}
\label{threshold:energy-entropy}

For any subset $C_n\subseteq\{-1,1\}^n$, consider a two-level energy
$H_n=\Delta_n\mathbf1_{C_n^c}$.  Its exact code-sector mass is
\[
 p_{\beta,n}=\mu_{\beta,n}(C_n)
 =\frac1{1+\exp\{S_n-\beta\Delta_n\}},
 \qquad
 S_n=\log\frac{2^n-|C_n|}{|C_n|}.
 \tag{EE}
\]
The scale $S_n/\Delta_n$ is where the sector energy benefit compensates
its entropy deficit; a bounded change in $\beta\Delta_n-S_n$ changes
its mass by a constant amount.  This is an exact calculation, not a
universal criterion for AA.  The uniform law on $C_n$ must separately
have a query obstruction.  For example, choosing $C_n$ to be a pair of
constant strings gives a readily identifiable sector rather than the
hidden-character separation.

For the character code of Section~\ref{qm:model-and-theorem},
$|C_n|=n$, $\delta_n=n/2^n$, and
$\Delta_n=\log(1+1/\delta_n)$ yield the exact decomposition
\[
 \mu_n=(1-w_n)U_n+w_nU_{C_n},\qquad
 w_n=\frac{\delta_n(e^{\beta_n\Delta_n}-1)}
 {1+\delta_n(e^{\beta_n\Delta_n}-1)}.
 \tag{EW}
\]
At fixed $\beta<1$ the hard mass is exponentially small and full AA
holds; at fixed $\beta\ge1$ it is bounded below and full AA fails.
The exact fixed-temperature result is already established in that
section.  The following refinement explains why merely showing
$w_n\to0$ is insufficient for arbitrary temperature schedules.

\begin{theorem}[Exact criterion for arbitrary character-code weights]
\label{threshold:weight-criterion}
Assume uniform-input AA.  For any sequence $w_n\in[0,1]$, on
$n=2^m$ with even $m$, the family
$(1-w_n)U_n+w_nU_{C_n}$ has property $\mathcal A$ if and only if there
exist constants $a,C>0$ such that
\[
 \log_2 n\le Cw_n^{-a}\qquad\hbox{whenever }w_n>0.
 \tag{WC}
\]
No restriction is needed at indices with $w_n=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For necessity, the one-query witness $P$ from the character-code
section satisfies, for every depth-$t$ classical tree,
\[
 \mathbb E_{\mu_n}|D-P|
 \ge\frac{w_n}{2}(1-2^{t-m/2}).
\]
Accuracy $\epsilon_n=w_n/8$ therefore requires
$t>m/2-2$.  A fixed polynomial simulation bound at $T=1$ implies (WC),
after changing constants to cover small $m$.

For sufficiency, fix $T,\epsilon$ and a quantum acceptance function $Q$.
If $w_n\le\epsilon/2$, use its uniform-AA simulator with uniform
mean error $\epsilon/2$; the mixture error is at most $\epsilon$.
If $w_n>\epsilon/2$, (WC) gives
$m\le C(2/\epsilon)^a$.  Query the $m$ positions indexed by the
standard basis vectors of $\mathbb F_2^m$.  These answers determine
a unique candidate character $c_{\widehat a}$.  Query
$k=\lceil\log_2(4/\epsilon)\rceil$ distinct remaining positions and
check agreement with that character.  If fewer than $k$ positions
remain, read the whole input and evaluate $Q$ exactly; this costs at
most $m+k$ queries.

Otherwise, on passing all checks output $Q(c_{\widehat a})$; on
failing, run a uniform-AA simulator $D_U$ with mean error
$\epsilon/4$.  On a true codeword the output is exact.  Under uniform
input the checked coordinates remain independent uniform signs after
the basis answers have been fixed, so the pass probability is exactly
$2^{-k}\le\epsilon/4$.  The uniform mean error of this procedure is
at most
$\mathbb E_U|D_U-Q|+\Pr_U(\mathrm{pass})\le\epsilon/2$.
The cost is
\[
 C(2/\epsilon)^a+\lceil\log_2(4/\epsilon)\rceil
 +S_0(T,4/\epsilon),
\]
where $S_0$ is the uniform-AA simulation polynomial.  This proves
$\mathcal A$.  Computation of $Q$ on a fully known candidate incurs
no input-query cost.
\end{proof}

For a near-critical schedule
$\beta_n=1-s_n/\Delta_n$ with $s_n\ge0$ and $s_n=o(n)$,
(EW) gives $w_n\asymp e^{-s_n}$ for all sufficiently large $n$.
Thus if $s_n=o(\log\log n)$, full AA still fails, even though $w_n$
may tend to zero.  If $s_n\ge c\log\log n$ for some fixed $c>0$
eventually, full AA holds.  The exact criterion (WC) covers irregular
schedules.  The fixed-mass thermodynamic window is $\Theta(1/n)$;
the polynomial-query property for a varying-temperature family asks
the finer question of how sector mass compares with its query
hardness.  These are different statements.
