\subsection{A general obstruction: a spread-out observable distinguishes
phases}\label{proof:local_interactions:a-general-obstruction-a-spread-out-observable-distinguishes-phases}

\textbf{Proposition L1.} Let \(\mu _n\) be any spin distribution.
Suppose there are real vectors \(a^{(n)}\) with \(\sum_i|a_i|\le 1\),
\(\max _i|a_i|\to 0\), and

\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_n}\left(\sum_i a_iX_i\right)>0.
\]

Then the family fails influence AA, and in fact fails every positive
dimension-free influence guarantee based on degree and a positive lower
bound for the variance.

\textbf{Proof.} The linear function \(f_n(x)=\sum_i a_ix_i\) is bounded
by one on the entire cube and has \(I_i(f_n)=a_i^{2}\) under every law.
Its maximum influence tends to zero while its variance is bounded below.
\qed

For symmetric zero-field systems the most direct case is \(a_i=1/n\).
More generally \(a_i=\sigma _i/n\), \(\sigma_i\in\{-1,1\}\), detects a
staggered or gauge-transformed phase. The substantive condition is a
collective random phase with a distributed linear witness. Concentration
on a single configuration alone does not satisfy it, because then the
variance disappears.

\textbf{Proposition L1a (a degree-one positive criterion).} If the spin
covariance matrix satisfies
\(\lVert \operatorname{Cov}_\mu (X)\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\le C\) uniformly
in n, then every globally bounded linear polynomial obeys

\[
\max_i I_i^\mu(f)\ge \operatorname{Var}_\mu(f)^2/C^2.
\]

Indeed, writing \(f=a_{0}+\sum_i a_iX_i\), global boundedness gives
\(\sum_i|a_i|\le 1\). Thus
\(\operatorname{Var}(f)\le C\sum_i a_i^{2}\le C \max _i|a_i|\), and
\(I_i(f)=a_i^{2}\). This is an actual positive theorem for degree one,
not a claim for arbitrary low degree. Bounded covariance norm is not
necessary for full AA: at critical Curie--Weiss the covariance norm
grows on the order of \(\sqrt{n}\), yet the polynomial AA theorem in the
main investigation holds with variance exponent four. Diverging
susceptibility therefore does not by itself prove AA failure.
