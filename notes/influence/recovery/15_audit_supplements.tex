\subsection{Additional normalization, potential, and robustness checks}
\label{influence-recovery:audit-supplements}

This subsection retains the additional estimates in the original
independent audit. Unless a different range is stated, \(f\in[0,1]\),
and compositions and squares are multilinearized on the cube.
The dossier uses \(I_i(f)=\mathbb E(D_i f)^2\), one quarter of the
unnormalized squared-flip influence in the imported exposition.
This affects only absolute constants.

\paragraph{A sharper pointwise budget.}
At a vertex \(x\), split coordinates according to the sign of
\(x_iD_i f(x)\). For either group \(S\), move exactly its coordinates
along the line from \(x\) to their opposites. The multilinear extension
of \(f\) on this line is a degree-at-most-\(d\) polynomial in \([0,1]\)
on \([0,1]\). Endpoint Markov bounds its derivative in absolute value
by \(d^2\); that derivative at zero is
\(-2\sum_{i\in S}x_iD_i f(x)\). Adding the two bounds gives
\[
 \sum_i|D_i f(x)|\le d^2,
 \qquad
 \sum_i(D_i f(x))^2\le d^2/2,
\]
where the last step uses \(|D_i f|\le1/2\). Thus
\(A_\mu(f)\le d^2/2\) for every law, before or after restriction.
This sharper normalization is compatible with the conservative
\(4d^2\) budget for \([-1,1]\)-valued functions above.

\paragraph{The fair-bit source specialization.}
For a degree-\(D\), \([0,1]\)-valued function \(g\) on independent fair
bits, Fourier orthogonality gives
\[
 J_j(g)=\sum_{S\ni j}\widehat g(S)^2,
 \qquad
 \Phi(g)=\sum_jJ_j(g)\le D\operatorname{Var}(g)\le D/4.
\]
The average potential drop when querying \(j\) is exactly \(J_j(g)\),
even when the immediately revealed variance is zero, as for parity
before all its coordinates have been read. Query the most influential
bit at every nonconstant leaf. Uniform AA and Jensen give
\[
 \mathbb E\Phi_t-\mathbb E\Phi_{t+1}
 \ge cV_t^{5/2}/D^6,
\]
where \(V_t\) is expected residual variance. Since \(V_t\) is
nonincreasing, more than \(D^7/(4ce^5)\) rounds force \(V_t\le e^2\).
Conditional-mean output then has mean absolute error at most \(e\).
For \([-1,1]\)-valued functions replace the initial \(D/4\) budget by
\(D\), or rescale. This specialization needs no dyadic-rounding logarithm.

\paragraph{Variance stability and the posterior transcript.}
For \([0,1]\)-valued random variables \(A=f(X)\), \(B=g(Z)\), if
\(\mathbb E|A-B|\le\delta\), then
\[
 |\operatorname{Var}A-\operatorname{Var}B|\le2\delta.
\]
Apply the independent-copy identity for variance and
\(|a^2-b^2|\le2|a-b|\) for \(a,b\in[-1,1]\).
The conditional per-site discrepancy hypothesis in (R) is essential;
unconditional coordinate-error bounds alone do not imply its
fractional-certificate estimate. Likewise, an exact globally consistent
oracle for \(Z\mid X=x\) supplies a fixed logical source tree with its
product prior after averaging over \(X\). This does not make source bits
independent conditional on the entire physical transcript. Incidental
physical observations must remain outside the logical source selection
rule. Fair-bit rounding can have large conditional error after a rare
output observation; the atom-independent block argument avoids this issue.
