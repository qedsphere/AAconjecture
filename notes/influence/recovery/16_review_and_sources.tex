\subsection{Sources, independent reviews, and finite verification}
\label{influence-recovery:review-and-verification}

The imported uniform influence inequality is
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09773}{Turturean, Theorem 1.1}.
The fractional-certificate input is
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11626v1}{Blanc--Docter--Strassle--Tan,
Fact 2.4}, also recorded in the supplied
\href{https://guyblanc.github.io/AA_Exposition.pdf}{exposition}.
The original audit records checking the uniform bound directly on
14 November 2026. The slice estimates are rederived above from
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2609.35327v1}{Girish--Mittal--Nehoran--Raz,
Lemmas 4.6 and 4.9--4.10}. All generalized inequalities here are
deductions using those stated imports.

The original audit records independent agent checks of both complete
proofs, with no substantive gap found. For the exchangeable proof the
checks covered deletion/addition of a uniformly chosen spin in adjacent
slices; the endpoint-uniform \(4r^2/N\) comparison; its validity for a
polynomial or coordinate selected after the prefix; the constants
\(24d^2b_H\), \(16d^2b_H\), and expected error \(36d^2/\sqrt L\);
the absence of an atom-floor factor; the exact potential identity and
budget under pinnings; and the final exponents.

The fixed-species extension was checked separately. Absolute differences
across an \(i\)-edge do not depend on its current \(i\)-spin, so the
product-expectation derivative estimate, including degenerate biases,
has no dimension factor. Conditional on a complete species-weight
vector, the remaining slice factors are independent after the fixed
prefixes. Telescoping before averaging permits arbitrarily correlated
weight vectors; reading small species fully causes no difficulty.
The error \(36rd^2/\sqrt L\), prefix cost \(O(r^3d^{18}/v^7)\),
and final \(O(r^3d^{27}\epsilon^{-21})\) bound were checked. Dependence
on a growing species count must remain explicit.

The product-block review checked dominated dyadic rounding even with
zero atoms; the comparison of fair-bit and block-resampling influences;
and the low-degree univariate interpolation of block laws. Indeed,
variance itself has degree at most \(2\Delta\) and range \([0,1/4]\),
allowing a stronger Markov bound than the conservative estimate in (B4).
The Hoeffding identity, convexity calculation, fixed-depth iteration,
posterior transfer, and forest and bounded-degree dynamics applications
were also checked, including the requirement to ignore incidental
physical observations. These are mathematical checks by another agent,
not formal verification or publication review.

The archived verification script records 99 exact conditional-potential
identities using rational arithmetic; the singleton-reveal obstruction;
a frozen block with zero physical influence but positive source influence;
an exact forest posterior; and finite Curie--Weiss identities. All recorded
checks passed. These finite checks corroborate the identities and do not
replace the proofs. The original Markdown, generated reports, scripts,
and numerical artifacts are archived outside the notes; this TeX edition
preserves the complete two mathematical source notes, the audit's unique
estimates and Gibbs construction, and their provenance.
