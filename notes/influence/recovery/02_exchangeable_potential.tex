\subsection{Exact potential identity and dimension-free
budget}\label{influence-recovery:exact-potential-identity-and-dimension-free-budget}

For an arbitrary input law, derivatives in coordinates \(j\ne i\)
commute with restriction of coordinate \(i\). Therefore \[
 \mathbb E_{X_i} A_{\mu\mid X_i}(f\mid X_i)
 =A_\mu(f)-I_i^\mu(f).                                   \tag{3}
\] Together with total variance, telescoping along an adaptive query
tree gives the exact identity \[
 \mathcal J_\mu(f;\mathcal T)
 =\operatorname{Var}_\mu(\mathbb E[f\mid H])
  +\mathbb E\sum_{t<\operatorname{depth}(H)}
       I_{i_t}^{\mu_{H_t}}(f_{H_t}).                       \tag{4}
\] Equivalently, the second term is
\(\mathbb E_X\sum_{i\text{ queried on }X}(D_i f(X))^2\); the tree never
needs to query a coordinate twice. In particular it is nonnegative.
Moreover, for every input law, \[
 0\le\Psi_\mu(f)\le1+4d^2.                               \tag{5}
\] For completeness, let \(g=(f+1)/2\). At a fixed vertex \(x\), flip
each coordinate in a subset \(S\) independently with probability \(t\).
The expected value \(P(t)\) of \(g\) is a polynomial of degree at most
\(d\), bounded in \([0,1]\) for \(t\in[0,1]\). Markov's inequality gives
\[
 \left|\sum_{i\in S}(g(x^{\oplus i})-g(x))\right|
 =|P'(0)|\le2d^2.
\] Apply this separately to positive and negative differences. Since
each difference has magnitude at most one,
\(\sum_i(D_i f(x))^2=\sum_i(g(x^{\oplus i})-g(x))^2\le4d^2\). Averaging
proves (5). Independence is not used in this section.
